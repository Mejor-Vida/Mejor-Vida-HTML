Final expense insurance (burial insurance)

Final expense insurance is typically a whole life policy for about $5,000 to $50,000, designed to help your family pay for a funeral, cremation, and related costs. Mejor Vida Insurance quotes quality carrier options.

How final expense insurance works

You choose a coverage amount, answer health questions (depending on the product), and pay a fixed premium. When you pass away, the insurer pays the death benefit in cash to your beneficiary. That money can cover funeral costs, medical bills, travel for family, or other needs — it is not locked to a funeral home.

  • Typical face amounts: $5,000 to $25,000 (sometimes up to $40,000–$50,000).
  • Usually whole life: coverage does not “expire” at a set age if premiums stay current.
  • Often no medical exam; carriers use a questionnaire and database checks.

Two main coverage paths

Most people quote one of these options:

Level / simplified issue (health questions)

You answer a health questionnaire. If you qualify, you often get full benefits from day one (or a short waiting period, depending on the product). This usually costs less than guaranteed acceptance.

Guaranteed acceptance

Few or no health questions. Almost always includes a two-year waiting period for natural death; if death occurs during the wait, the family typically receives premiums paid plus interest. Costs more per dollar of coverage.

You may also see plans aimed at cremation or lower face amounts. Mejor Vida Insurance explains which path fits your health and budget.

Immediate benefit vs. waiting period

“No waiting period” generally means that, once the policy is in force and no exclusion applies, your beneficiary can receive the full covered amount for a covered death from the start. Guaranteed-acceptance plans almost always have a two-year wait for natural causes. Some level policies also have first-year rules — always review the contract and ask before you sign.

How much final expense insurance costs

Many people pay roughly $30 to $100 per month for about $10,000 of coverage, depending on age, sex, health, and tobacco use. Whole life final expense premiums are usually level if you keep the policy in force.

How to apply for final expense insurance

Applying for final expense coverage is usually straightforward. Most applications ask for basic personal details: full name, date of birth, address, Social Security number, phone number, beneficiaries, and how you’ll pay premiums.

What changes by product is health underwriting. No-waiting-period (or short-wait) plans almost always include a health questionnaire and a current medication list. Guaranteed acceptance policies typically skip health questions, but they usually cost more and include a two-year waiting period for natural death.

There are two common ways to buy coverage. Knowing the difference helps you avoid limiting your options:

Buying directly from an insurance company

Some people try to enroll by mail, website, or phone with no agent. In practice, many final expense carriers do not sell that way to the public. When they do, the product is often guaranteed acceptance: more expensive per dollar of coverage and, almost always, with a two-year wait. You also may not have a dedicated agent if service issues come up later.

Working with an independent agency (like Mejor Vida)

You speak with a licensed agent — by phone, WhatsApp, or online — who reviews your age, health, budget, and state to compare multiple carriers. If you’re approved, the insurer usually mails the policy to you. Using an independent agent does not add a separate fee; the carrier sets the premium. Nearly all final expense products are sold through licensed agents. If you refuse to talk with an agent, you shut yourself out of much of the market.

If you want coverage for a parent or grandparent, that’s fine — but the insured person must take part. They have to answer any health questions and sign the application, even if you hold power of attorney.

With Mejor Vida the typical flow is: get a quote or call Mejor Vida Insurance → compare options → complete the application (phone or e-signature) → receive the policy and confirm beneficiaries and payment. It’s usually faster than fully underwritten life insurance with a medical exam; exact timing depends on the carrier and database checks.

How do you file a claim?

Filing a final expense claim works like other life insurance. The beneficiary notifies the insurer of the death. The company sends (by mail, fax, or email) a claim form to sign and return, along with a copy of the death certificate. Those documents let the carrier process and pay the death benefit.

A helpful option for families

Depending on the company and the situation, the beneficiary may receive the full benefit, or ask that part of it go straight to the funeral home with the rest paid to the family. Julie can help your beneficiaries know whom to call and which documents to gather.

Final expense policies are often paid relatively quickly once the claim is approved — frequently within days — while some traditional life claims can take longer. Keep the policy and policy numbers somewhere your family can find them.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as a prepaid funeral?

No. Final expense insurance pays cash to your beneficiary. A prepaid funeral is usually tied to a funeral home. See our guide on final expense vs. prepaid funerals.

Do I need a medical exam?

For most final expense plans, no. Carriers use health questions and databases. Guaranteed acceptance skips the exam but usually costs more and includes a waiting period.
