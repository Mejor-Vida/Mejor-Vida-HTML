Types of Life Insurance Explained

There are three main families of life insurance: whole life, term life, and (in some markets) universal life. There are also many specialties—final expense, cremation, guaranteed acceptance, mortgage protection, and more.

Each type has its own features, benefits, and cost. Ultimately, all types pay a cash death benefit to beneficiaries, who can use the money for funeral costs, medical bills, income replacement, or other needs.

Whole Life Insurance

Whole life insurance is a permanent cash-value policy. Premiums and the death benefit are typically guaranteed to stay level, while cash value grows tax-deferred. Owners may borrow or withdraw from cash value according to policy rules.

Key Features

  • Lifetime coverage that is designed to last forever.
  • Cash value growth you can borrow from as it accumulates.
  • Rates that are typically locked in for life.

Term Life Insurance

Term life insurance covers a set period—often 10–30 years or until a stated age. When the term ends, coverage ends unless you convert or renew under policy options.

Key Features

  • Often the most affordable coverage per dollar.
  • Best for temporary needs like debt or income replacement.
  • Underwriting is typically stricter than final expense products.

Final Expense Insurance

Final expense insurance—also called burial or funeral insurance—is a specialty whole life policy with simplified underwriting, designed to cover end-of-life expenses.

Key Features

  • No medical exam in most cases; pre-existing conditions often still qualify.
  • Claims are typically paid quickly once approved.
  • Affordable premiums that never increase and lifelong coverage.

Guaranteed Acceptance

Guaranteed acceptance has no exam and no health questions. In exchange, there is usually a waiting period (often 24 months) where a natural-cause death may only refund premiums paid plus interest.

Key Features

  • Cannot be denied for prior medical issues.
  • Lifetime coverage with level premiums.
  • Fast approvals because there is no medical underwriting.

No-Waiting-Period Insurance

No-waiting-period policies provide full coverage for natural and accidental death from day one (subject to standard exclusions such as suicide during an early period).

Key Features

  • Full day-one coverage for covered causes of death.
  • Immediate peace of mind that the benefit can be used when needed.
  • No exam required; many carriers accept a wide range of conditions.

Cremation Insurance

Cremation insurance is a smaller whole life benefit meant primarily to cover cremation costs and related final arrangements.

Key Features

  • No exam; approvals are often instant or within a few business days.
  • Fast claim payouts.
  • Permanent coverage at a fixed rate.

Instant Life Insurance

Instant life insurance can be purchased online or by phone without a medical exam. Many applications are approved instantly or within a few business days after basic health questions.

Key Features

  • Fast coverage when you need protection quickly.
  • Usually based on health questions, not an exam.
  • Most approved policies have no waiting period.

Mortgage Protection

Mortgage protection is meant to cover the mortgage balance if the homeowner dies. If the death benefit exceeds the balance, remaining funds typically stay with beneficiaries.

Key Features

  • Often available without a medical exam.
  • Large benefits available for high mortgage balances.
  • Flexible funds—they do not have to go only to the lender.

Children's Life Insurance

Children's life insurance is usually whole life with a low locked-in premium and cash value. As adults, many products allow buying more coverage without new medical qualification.

Key Features

  • Very low locked-in premium for the child’s lifetime.
  • Can often be purchased entirely online.
  • Builds cash value for future expenses like college or a home.

Who Should Get Life Insurance?

Life insurance is worth it if your death would have a harmful financial impact on a loved one. If you’re the primary income earner and have dependents, you need coverage to replace income. If you have no other means to pay final expenses, a policy keeps funeral bills from burdening your family. If you have a car note or mortgage, life insurance can help pay those balances off.

Which type should you choose?

Ask what the death benefit would be used for. Long-term needs → permanent or final expense. Temporary needs (mortgage, income) → term. A licensed Mejor Vida agent helps you compare options in Nebraska, Kansas, Colorado, and Nevada.

In general, choose a permanent policy for long-term needs such as estate planning or final expenses. Choose term coverage for temporary needs such as a mortgage or income replacement. Financial experts consistently caution that it is unwise to shop for life insurance solely on price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are taxes due on a life insurance payout?

In nearly all circumstances, the money paid out by a life insurance policy does not create a tax burden for the beneficiaries. According to the IRS, taxes may become due when interest is accrued or if the policy was transferred in exchange for money or other valuable consideration.

How much does life insurance cost?

Cost depends on age, health, amount, and policy type. Women often pay a bit less than men of the same age for the same coverage. Use our free quote to see real prices for your profile.

Can someone get life insurance with pre-existing conditions?

Often yes. Simplified issue, no-exam, and guaranteed acceptance options exist. A licensed agent helps match you to the right plan based on your medical history.

What is the best type of life insurance?

The “best” type is the one that meets your goal: permanent for long-term needs, term for temporary debts or income. Compare features and cost together—do not shop on price alone.

How many beneficiaries can you have on a life insurance policy?

Most policies allow multiple primary and contingent beneficiaries with percentage shares (for example 50/50). Your agent can set them up correctly.

Can seniors get life insurance?

Yes. Final expense and guaranteed acceptance products are designed for older applicants. With the carriers we work with, applications can go up to age 85 or 89 (for example Aetna), depending on the product. Eligibility and price depend on age and health.

Do I need life insurance if I have it through my employer?

Employer group coverage often ends or shrinks when you leave the job, and the amount may not be enough to replace income or cover final expenses. Many families add an individual policy they own and control.

What is face value in life insurance?

Face value (also called the face amount) is the dollar amount the insurer pays beneficiaries when the insured dies, according to the policy terms. It is the death benefit amount stated on the policy.

Are life insurance policies part of an estate?

Usually the death benefit is paid directly to named beneficiaries and does not go through probate. In some situations, policy value may still count toward the gross estate for federal estate-tax purposes. Ask an attorney or tax advisor for your situation.

What exclusions are there for life insurance?

Common exclusions include suicide during an early period (often two years) and the incontestability clause: after that period, the insurer generally cannot cancel coverage for misstatements on the application except for fraud. Always read the policy for exact exclusions.

How do you cancel life insurance?

You can request cancellation in writing from the insurer or through your agent. Stopping premium payments may cause the policy to lapse under contract rules. On cash-value policies, ask about surrender value or other options before you cancel. In some cases you may also transfer or replace a policy—speak with a licensed agent before you do.
