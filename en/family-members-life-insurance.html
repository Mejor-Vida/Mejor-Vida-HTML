Life insurance for a relative who is not your parent: owner lists and beneficiary lists are not the same

An aunt, a cousin, or an in-law may be someone whose funeral you would pay. That can meet the NAIC’s insurable interest. What changes is who may own the contract. At Mutual of Omaha, aunts, nephews, and cousins appear as accepted contingent beneficiaries, not on the short owner list. Each company publishes its own. Mejor Vida Insurance confirms it before you apply.

Three facts to start with

  • The insured person always signs. There is no adult policy “without them knowing.”
  • There is no group burial plan that covers several adults on one contract. Each life, one policy.
  • A child or grandchild rider on your own policy is the exception: it covers minors, not your aunt.

If the relative is a parent, a grandparent, or a sibling, use those guides: the owner rules are clearer.

Relationships companies usually recognize

The NAIC talks about immediate family, and sometimes a partner or a creditor. On final expense, a funeral you would pay is the usual argument. A neighbor or a friend, with no financial loss, does not qualify.

Who can own the policy, and who can only receive the check

On Mutual of Omaha simplified life, not every relative can run the contract. The three levels go from the most control to the least. Other companies keep their own lists.

  1. 1 Most control

    Owner or beneficiary

    Can run the contract — change beneficiaries, ask the company questions — and can also receive the check.

    Typically accepted

    • Spouse
    • Fiancé
    • Domestic partner
    • Parent
    • Adult child
    • Trust
    • Business partner (with paperwork)

  2. 2 Receives, cannot own

    Beneficiary only

    Can receive the payment. Cannot own the policy or discuss the contract with the company.

    Typically accepted

    • Siblings
    • Grandparents and grandchildren
    • Relatives by marriage
    • An ex-spouse
    • The estate

  3. 3 Backup

    Contingent beneficiary

    Paid only if the primary beneficiary has already died. Not an owner.

    Typically accepted

    • Aunts
    • Uncles
    • Nieces
    • Nephews
    • Cousins and other more distant relatives
    • Friends

Other appointed companies (Transamerica, Aetna Accendo, Americo, Corebridge) keep their own lists. A funeral amount is usually simpler than a large term policy. We confirm the product — not a generic internet “yes.”

Why there is no “one policy for the whole family”

Each adult is a different health risk. Premiums, questions, and waits do not blend. What does exist: child riders on an adult policy, and separate children’s policies. That does not cover your 62-year-old sister.

How to start if the relationship is more distant

Tell us the exact relationship and the amount. If the product will not let you own it, there are two honest paths: the relative owns it and you pay (with the risk you cannot ask questions), or it is not issued and you save. Do not sign an online form in someone else’s name: that can be a crime and it voids the contract.

Frequently asked questions

Can I insure an aunt or uncle?

Sometimes, if there is insurable interest (for example, you would pay the funeral) and they sign. You may only be allowed as beneficiary, not owner. We confirm with the company.

What about a close friend?

Without a documented financial loss, the NAIC says a stranger cannot buy. A friend is usually out, unless there is a loan or a formal business.

Does workplace group life cover several relatives?

Group life covers the employee, and sometimes a spouse or children, according to that plan. It does not replace a final expense policy on an aunt.
