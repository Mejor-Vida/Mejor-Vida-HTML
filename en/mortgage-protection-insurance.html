Mortgage protection is life insurance for your home loan — not a different kind of policy

When someone asks for “mortgage protection insurance,” they almost always mean life insurance sized to the remaining home loan. If you die while you keep paying the policy, the person you named receives cash. That person can pay off the loan — or use the money another way. The bank is not paid automatically unless you signed an assignment: a written form that gives the bank the right to that money.

What to remember

  • There is no secret lender product. This is life insurance — usually term (coverage that lasts a set number of years, then ends) — with an amount and a length chosen to match the mortgage.
  • The check goes to the beneficiary (the person you name), not to the bank, unless you signed a form that gives the bank that right. If the home loan is smaller than the policy amount, the rest usually stays with the family.
  • It is not private mortgage insurance (PMI). PMI protects the lender if you stop making payments. The U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) is blunt: PMI does not protect you.

After you buy or refinance, mail often arrives offering “mortgage protection.” That is not a bank program. It is life insurance advertising. Compare it with an independent quote before you reply.

What you are actually buying

A mortgage is a debt with an end date: a number of years and a balance that falls if you pay. Life insurance does not have to be labeled “mortgage” to do the job. What matters is that the amount covers what is still owed — or several years of the payment — and that the term lasts as long as the loan has left.

Mejor Vida Insurance quotes that with term life from insurance companies we work with. A permanent policy (whole life) can also pay a balance, but it costs more per dollar because the coverage does not expire. For a debt that does expire, term is usually the cheaper path.

What you buy

A life insurance amount — for example $150,000 or $250,000 — that lasts a term you choose. On our term quotes the usual lengths are 10, 15, 20, 25, or 30 years.

Who gets paid

The person or people you name as beneficiary. The lender is not on the contract unless you assign it in writing.

What you do not buy

It does not pay the mortgage if you lose a job or get sick, unless a separate add-on says so — and those add-ons are rarely on these policies. It also does not replace homeowners insurance or PMI.

How the payout works, in four steps

  1. You choose the amount and the years A clear way: look at today’s balance and the years left. If you owe $220,000 with 18 years remaining, a 20-year $250,000 term covers the balance with a cushion. If the goal is that a spouse can keep making the monthly payment, the amount can be lower — several years of that payment — and there is a design that shrinks over time built for that.
  2. You answer health questions There is no term life with no health questions. There is a questionnaire. A simplified plan (no in-office exam) exists: American Amicable Easy Term returns a decision in the same session, from $25,000 up to $500,000 before age 46 and up to $300,000 after. A full health review — Transamerica Trendsetter Super, Corebridge Select-a-Term, Mutual of Omaha Term Life Answers — usually opens a better price and higher amounts.
  3. You pay a fixed monthly amount for the years you bought On level term, the monthly payment does not change if you keep the policy active, and the amount the family receives does not change either. On decreasing term, the monthly payment usually stays the same while the payout falls with the years, the way a loan balance falls.
  4. If you die, the family receives cash The insurer pays the beneficiary, not the mortgage. That person can pay off the loan, keep making the payment, or use the money for a funeral and the month’s bills. If the balance is already smaller than the policy amount, the extra does not go to the bank.

This is not private mortgage insurance (PMI) — and not homeowners insurance

Three coverages get mixed up at closing and they do different jobs. The CFPB (the federal consumer-protection agency) keeps them separate. It helps to keep them straight before you buy a fourth thing with a similar name.

The CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau) describes PMI this way: the lender requires it when the down payment is under 20% on a conventional loan, and it insures the lender if you stop paying. If you fall behind, you can still lose the home. PMI does not pay a death benefit.

Term life is what covers a loan — two designs

A mortgage has an end date. The insurance that covers it should last at least as long as the years left on the loan, not forever. That product is term life: not a special bank policy, and not PMI.

At companies we work with, term comes in two designs. The payout stays the same for the whole term, or it shrinks as the balance falls. Whole life is a different product, and it gets offered a lot at closing. It is usually the wrong first tool if the only job is the remaining loan.

The typical first quote

Level term

The amount paid to the family and the monthly price stay the same for the whole term. If the balance has already fallen and you die near the end, the family still receives the full amount — not only what is still owed. That extra is theirs. This is the design we quote in the term quote tool with Transamerica, Corebridge, and Mutual of Omaha.

If you only want the shrinking balance

Decreasing term

The payout falls on a schedule, meant to track a loan. It costs less than level term because the company covers less over time. Americo Payment Protector can pay monthly income for the remaining period (at least 24 months) or a lump sum. Typical starting amounts run $25,000 to $450,000. Periods are 15, 20, 25, 30 years and to age 70. The monthly payment does not increase. Payment Protector Continuation leaves 10% of the original amount as permanent coverage if monthly income was not used. Americo.

What about whole life?

It lasts for life if you pay on time, and that is why it costs more per dollar. It fits if, besides the mortgage, you want a benefit that does not expire — a spouse, final expenses, a gift. It is not the first path if the only job is a balance that will end on its own.

The mail that arrives after closing

Almost no one closes on a house without getting letters about “mortgage protection” or a “special program.” That is not a lender benefit. PMI is arranged by the bank and shows on your Loan Estimate (the cost form the lender must give you when you close). This letter does not.

If you reply, an agent will try to sell you life insurance. It might be a fair policy or an expensive, rigid one. The useful question is not “did this come from the bank?” — it did not. The useful question is whether the amount, the term, and the price hold up against an independent quote.

How much to buy, and for how many years

Do not use a number from an ad. Add up what the family would still need to cover if you died this year. The buyer’s guide from the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) asks for that personal add-up, not a single formula.

  1. Today’s balance That is the starting point if the goal is to leave the house free of debt. Not the purchase price from ten years ago.
  2. The years left The insurance term should last at least as long as the loan. If 22 years remain, a 20-year term ends first. A 30-year term costs more per month, because the company is covering a longer stretch.
  3. If income also has to be replaced Add several years of your pay to the mortgage. An example: $55,000 a year for ten years is $550,000; plus a $180,000 balance is about $730,000. Subtract savings or other policies. That is a conversation, not a quote.

A short example. Balance $200,000, 20 years left, non-smoker age 40. Does this path fit?

It can fit if

  • A balance or a monthly payment remains that the family could not carry without your income.
  • You want the money to go to a person you trust, not automatically to the bank.
  • You can answer health questions and a term length still exists at your age.

It usually does not fit if

  • The goal is a funeral, not a loan. Then compare final expense.
  • You cannot or will not answer health questions. Term is not issued with no questions.
  • The house is already paid off. Then the name “mortgage protection” does not describe what you need.

Where to buy it — and where not to

  1. An independent agent Mejor Vida Insurance compares insurance companies for your age, health, and term. That is the path we use.
  2. Straight from an insurer Some sell to the public. You see one price. You do not see whether another company we work with charges less for the same amount.
  3. The home-buying team The lender and the real-estate agent are not your life agent. They may recommend someone. Ask to quote separately, with the balance and the years in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Does the bank get the life insurance if I die?

No, unless there is an assignment (a written form that gives the bank the right to the money). The beneficiary — the person you named — receives the cash and decides whether to pay the loan. That is the rule on the policies we quote.

Is this the same as PMI (private mortgage insurance)?

No. Private mortgage insurance (PMI) protects the lender if you stop paying and is usually required with less than 20% down. Life insurance pays your family if you die. You can have both; one does not replace the other. Source: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB).

How much does it cost?

It depends on age, sex, tobacco, health, amount, and term. In our samples, $250,000 for 20 years at age 40 is about $16–$18 a month (non-tobacco, best health class). At 50, the same plan is about $31–$40. Use the tables on this page and the quote tool for your combination.

Until what age can I buy it?

It depends on the term. A 10-year term can be issued into about age 80; a 30-year term into about 60, non-tobacco. Where Mejor Vida Insurance is licensed is on the licenses page.

Does the mortgage get paid off by itself?

Only if the beneficiary uses the money that way, or if the policy was assigned to the lender. The life contract does not settle the loan on its own.

Do I have to buy the one that comes in the mail?

You are not required to. Compare the amount, the term, and the price with an independent quote. Letters after closing are not a bank program.

Can I use whole life for the mortgage?

Yes, and it costs more. It makes sense if you also want a benefit that does not expire. For the balance alone, term usually buys more dollars for the same monthly payment.

What if I finish paying the house early?

On level term the policy still runs to the end of the term, and the family would receive the full amount. You can cancel; there is usually no savings inside the policy to return. On decreasing term, the payout would already have fallen with the years.

Is there a two-year wait?

On a qualifying level term policy, no. The full amount applies once the policy is active, subject to the contract. The two-year wait is typical of guaranteed acceptance (a plan with no health questions), which is a different product and is not used for a large loan balance.
