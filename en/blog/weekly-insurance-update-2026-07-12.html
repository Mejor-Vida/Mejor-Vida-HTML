Weekly U.S. Life & Final Expense Insurance Update

📰 3 stories | 🏥 Long-term care | 📈 Medigap premiums | ⚠ Life settlements

Each week we summarize the most important insurance news to help families understand how these changes could affect their financial protection. This edition covers a major deal involving older long-term care policies, rising Medigap premiums, and what families should know before selling or dropping a life insurance policy.

Story 1: Unum Completes $3.8 Billion Long-Term Care Reinsurance Transaction with Fortitude Re

Source: Business Wire / Unum | Published: July 6, 2026

This week Unum — an insurer known for disability and long-term care products — announced a large deal: it moved the financial risk on about 50,000 older long-term care policies to another company, Fortitude Re. The transaction involves roughly $3.8 billion.

Why should you care? Many families have (or know someone with) an older long-term care policy. Those policies were often sold decades ago, before companies had as much real-world data on how expensive care would become. Over time, those promises got costlier than expected for insurers.

Does it affect you? If you have this kind of Unum policy, the key point is simple: Unum still administers the policy. You still pay premiums and file claims with Unum. The change is mainly a financial arrangement between companies — it should not change your coverage or how you ask for help.

Should you do anything? Usually you do not need an emergency call. Do keep your policy handy, make sure payments are current, and ask for a plain-language explanation if you get a confusing letter. Your insurance is still your insurance — but understanding the news helps you stay calm when headlines sound alarming.

Story 2: Medigap Premiums Jump 12% to 26% as Seniors Face Renewal Notices

Source: CBS News | Published: July 8, 2026

If you (or your parents) have a Medicare supplement — also called Medigap — this story is worth reading slowly. A CBS News report dated July 8, 2026 explains that many seniors are opening renewal notices with steep increases. Filings for popular plans such as Plan G often range, depending on the state and company, from about 12% to more than 26%. In some isolated cases, reported increases have been even higher.

Why is this happening? Medical costs rise, people use more services, and companies adjust pricing on older blocks of business. Not every company raises rates the same way, and two people with the same plan type can see very different numbers.

Should you stay put without looking? Not necessarily. Some states have a “birthday rule” that can make certain switches easier once a year. It is also wise not to decide on price alone: moving to Medicare Advantage may look cheaper, but it often comes with doctor networks and different rules — and switching back is not always easy.

The steady approach: compare carefully, ask what options exist in your state, and do not sign anything in a rush. Your coverage may still be solid — but a big increase is a signal to get informed, not to panic.

Story 3: NAIFA Issues Alert on Life Settlements and Urges Clear Education on Taxes and Alternatives

Source: InsuranceNewsNet / NAIFA | Published: July 11, 2026

Thinking about stopping life insurance payments, or cashing out because the premium no longer fits your budget? Before you decide, there is an option many families never hear about: selling the policy to an investor (often called a life settlement). NAIFA, an association of insurance advisors, issued an alert on July 11, 2026 asking that families get clear information before walking away from a policy.

In plain terms: someone may pay you a lump sum for your policy (often more than the cash surrender value, but less than the full death benefit). In return, they take over the premiums and receive the benefit when you pass away.

It sounds simple, but it is not. The money you receive can be taxed in layers. And if you may need Medicaid for long-term care, that lump sum can count as an asset and complicate eligibility. Many policies also include accelerated benefits for serious illness that can provide cash without selling everything.

What should you do today? If you were about to let a policy lapse, pause and ask for a full explanation of your options. Not every smaller policy finds a buyer. Getting informed can keep your family from losing value that is still available.
