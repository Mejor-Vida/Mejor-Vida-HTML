Before you sell or lapse a life policy: what NAIFA wants families to know

July 11, 2026 | Mejor Vida Insurance | About 8 min read

Thinking about stopping payments or taking cash from a life policy? NAIFA’s alert explains taxes, Medicaid risk, and alternatives before you decide.

Published: July 11, 2026 · Source: InsuranceNewsNet / NAIFA

Many families face a quiet hard moment: the life insurance premium no longer fits the budget, or someone says “just cash it out.” Before you let that policy go, NAIFA — an association of insurance advisors — issued an alert on July 11, 2026 asking that families learn every door, not only lapse or surrender.

What a life settlement is

In a life settlement, an investor pays you for your policy. You receive money now. They pay the premiums afterward and, when you pass away, they receive the benefit. That payment is often higher than the cash surrender value, but lower than the full death benefit.

For some seniors it can help. For others it is not the best option — or it is not available at all. Institutional buyers often prefer larger policies; smaller policies sometimes find no market.

Taxes, Medicaid, and other surprises

The money you receive is not always “clean.” Under current tax rules, parts of the payment can be treated differently (return of what you paid, ordinary income, and in some cases capital gain). If nobody walks you through a simple example, tax time can bring a surprise.

It also matters if you may need Medicaid for long-term care. A large payment can count as an asset and affect eligibility. In those situations, talk with an elder-law attorney before you sign.

Alternatives many families never hear about

Some policies include accelerated benefits: if a qualifying serious illness occurs, you may access part of the benefit while living. That is not the same as selling the whole policy, and it can sometimes meet a cash need without cutting all protection for the family.

NAIFA’s alert makes a human point: do not lapse a permanent policy only because “I can’t afford it” without first asking what other exits exist.

What you can do

  • If you are about to stop paying, pause for a week and ask for an options checklist.
  • Ask about accelerated benefits or other policy features.
  • If someone offers to buy the policy, get in writing how taxes work and what happens with Medicaid.
  • Do not sign under pressure. A permanent decision deserves time.
