Medigap premium increases: what they mean for you and your family

July 8, 2026 | Mejor Vida Insurance | About 8 min read

Many seniors are seeing renewal notices for their Medicare supplement. We explain why percentages vary and how to compare options without rushing.

Published: July 8, 2026 · Source: CBS News

Opening a renewal notice and seeing a big increase hurts — especially on a fixed budget. A CBS News report dated July 8, 2026 describes how many seniors are facing notable Medigap (Medicare Supplement) premium increases. The increases are not the same everywhere: they depend on the company, the plan, and the state. For popular plans such as Plan G, many reported filings often range from about 12% to more than 26%, and in isolated cases the numbers have been even higher.

Why two people can see different increases

Medigap helps cover costs that Original Medicare does not fully pay. When medical costs rise or people use more services, companies review pricing. Two neighbors with the “same” Plan G can have different insurers — and therefore different increases. A national headline does not mean your letter shows the exact same percentage.

Other 2026 Medicare costs can also add to the feeling that “everything went up,” including the Part B deductible. Separating the monthly premium from what you pay out of pocket makes comparisons clearer.

Should you switch plans immediately?

Not automatically. First, confirm what your notice says and any deadline. Then ask what options exist in your state. In some states, a “birthday rule” can allow certain switches near your birthday without starting over with medical questions. In other states, the rules are stricter.

Many families look at Medicare Advantage because it is sometimes advertised with a low premium. That can work for some people, but not for everyone: doctor networks, prior authorizations, and different rules are common. Going back to Medigap later is not always easy if your health has changed. Compare carefully — not just by the first number on the letter.

What a family can do

  • Read the full notice and write down the new premium and deadline.
  • Ask whether your state has a birthday rule or other switch windows.
  • Compare like-for-like plans before leaving a Medigap plan you already understand.
  • Avoid one-day decisions driven only by sticker shock.

Your coverage may still be a good fit. The goal is not panic — it is clear information and an informed choice.
