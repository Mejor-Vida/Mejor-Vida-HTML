Florida stops new sales, annuity rates, and how to read a whole life list

October 4, 2026 · News from September 27 through October 3, 2026 | Mejor Vida Insurance | About 6 min read

This week: Florida suspended new sales at one annuity company · posted fixed-annuity rates are high · a whole life list does not pick your policy

Each week we summarize insurance news so a family can see what happened and what to check at home. This edition covers three facts from October 1 and October 2.

Story 1: Florida ordered Atlantic Coast Life to stop new sales and renewals

Source: Florida Office of Insurance Regulation | Published: October 1, 2026

On October 1, 2026, the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation issued an immediate final order. That office watches insurers in Florida. The order suspends Atlantic Coast Life Insurance Company’s certificate of authority, the state’s permission to sell insurance there.

The office said the company is impaired: it does not meet the financial cushion state law requires. That is not, on this page, a bankruptcy judgment. The notice also says the South Carolina Department of Insurance, the home-state regulator, asked a court to place Atlantic Coast and a subsidiary into rehabilitation. Rehabilitation is a court process in which a person chosen through the state takes charge of a struggling insurer and tries to steady it. A petition is a request, not proof the judge has already finished that step.

The company must stop new sales and renewals in Florida. It must keep servicing policies already in force, and keep its duty to pay covered losses and unearned premium, the part of a payment that covers time still ahead. Servicing a policy is not a promise that every future claim will be paid in full. If your contract names another company, the order does not rewrite it. If page one says Atlantic Coast Life, talk with that company and your state insurance department before you add money. A state guaranty association is a backup fund that can cover certain benefits, up to a dollar cap in state law, if an insurer cannot pay.

Story 2: Posted fixed-annuity rates are up, and the term still matters

Source: Kiplinger | Published: October 2, 2026

On October 2, Kiplinger wrote that rates on new fixed annuities were at highs of many years. A fixed annuity is an insurance contract that credits a guaranteed interest rate for a written term. It is not a bank account. One form is a multi-year guaranteed annuity: it locks the rate for a term, often three to ten years. Growth can wait for taxes until withdrawal, under the contract and under tax rules.

Citing a list dated October 1, Kiplinger published 6.10% for three years, 6.55% for five years, 6.95% for seven years, and 6.35% for ten years as the highest figures on that list. Its example: $100,000 for five years at 6.55% credits $6,550 a year, and the principal comes back at the end if the contract is kept. That math is the magazine’s illustration, not an offer from Mejor Vida Insurance. The article also described the 10-year Treasury bond near 5.25%. That bond is a loan to the U.S. government. Its rate is not your annuity’s rate.

A high rate is not what you keep if you leave early. Ask about the surrender period, the time when a withdrawal can cost a charge, and whether there is a market-value adjustment, a clause that can change the amount on certain early withdrawals. Kiplinger warned that the guarantee depends on the company’s ability to pay, that rates change, and that an average is not every insurer.

Story 3: A list of the best whole life companies is a starting point

Source: Forbes Advisor | Reviewed: October 1, 2026

Forbes Advisor marked October 1, 2026, on its page of the best whole life insurance companies of 2026. Whole life is permanent coverage. If you pay as written, you generally keep a death benefit for life, a premium the contract can guarantee, and cash value. Cash value is money that builds inside the policy and that you can use under its rules, often with a loan or a withdrawal. It is not a separate checking account.

The page says it reviewed 26 large companies and looked at satisfaction, strength, past results, and price. It put Pacific Life first overall. One reason was a below-average complaint level at the National Association of Insurance Commissioners, the group of state insurance offices. That level compares complaints with the company’s size. Forbes put Northwestern Mutual first for financial strength, with a “superior” grade from AM Best. AM Best is an agency that uses letters, from D to A++, to estimate whether an insurer can pay claims. A high letter is not your personal approval.

The same page says one Pacific Life policy, Flex 16, does not pay dividends. A dividend, on a participating policy, is a refund the company may pay. Future dividends are not guaranteed. That is why the list does not choose your contract. Ask for the illustration, the paper with the guaranteed column and the projected column, and ask whether the dividend rate in the picture is a promise or an assumption.
