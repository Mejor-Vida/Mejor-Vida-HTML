How to get final expense insurance when you already have a health condition

A pre-existing condition is a diagnosis or treatment that was already there when you apply. On final expense that does not mean an automatic decline. Many people with diabetes, high blood pressure, or an old heart attack still qualify for a level plan: there are health questions, not an in-office exam, and the full benefit can apply from the first covered payment.

Three facts to start with

  • There are three paths: a level or immediate plan (questions, often no two-year wait), a graded or modified plan (limited benefit at first), and guaranteed acceptance (no questions, always a wait for natural death).
  • No company we quote offers zero questions and a full natural-death benefit from day one. Ads that say “no exam and no waiting period” almost always still have a questionnaire.
  • Mejor Vida Insurance compares appointed companies — Mutual of Omaha Living Promise, Accendo, Transamerica Immediate Solution, Americo, and, when needed, Corebridge GIWL — using your age, tobacco, and real answers, not a generic internet list.

Start with the questionnaire. Guaranteed acceptance is plan B when that questionnaire cannot issue a day-one benefit. It is not the first try for diabetes or high blood pressure.

Three policy types, in plain language

The brand name changes. What matters is when the company pays and what you have to answer.

Level or immediate

There are questions and a review of prescriptions and databases. If you qualify, the full amount can apply for covered natural death from the first payment. It is usually the lowest price per dollar. Living Promise Level issues ages 45–85, up to about $50,000. Accendo Level can issue through 89 ($25,000 cap at 76–89). Transamerica Immediate Solution goes through 85.

Graded or modified

There are still questions. In the first years, a non-accidental death may pay only part of the face amount or return premiums plus contract interest. Accendo Modified, for example, typically returns 110% of earned premiums in years 1–2 for non-accidental death and the full amount from year 3. A covered accident can pay in full from the start.

Guaranteed acceptance

There are no health questions. Within the age and amount, you are not declined for medical history. There is always about a two-year wait for non-accidental death. Corebridge GIWL: ages 50–80, $5,000–$25,000; during the wait, 110% of premiums paid. See guaranteed acceptance.

There is no “guaranteed approval with no waiting period”

If the company knows nothing about your health, it cannot pay $10,000 or $25,000 after collecting a few premiums. That is why guaranteed acceptance carries a wait. The path to a day-one benefit is a plan with questions you can answer without hitting that product’s knockouts — or, on some products, a “yes” that still leaves an immediate plan at another rate class.

How we find the product that fits

There is no “best company for everyone.” There is a company whose questionnaire matches your history. The same diagnosis can be level at one company and GIWL-only at another.

  1. Facts, not guesses. Age, height, weight, tobacco, medications, hospital stays, and oxygen if it was prescribed. A “no” that should have been “yes” can void a claim.
  2. Several appointed companies. The Living Promise questions are not Accendo’s and not Transamerica’s. That is why we compare, instead of sending one blind application.
  3. Then the price. Price follows age, sex, tobacco, and class (Preferred, Standard, tobacco). Health decides the class and whether there is a wait; we do not invent a separate discount.

Coverage by condition

These guides use the rules of the companies we quote. This is not a 60-disease directory: these are the situations that show up most on final expense and that we have underwriting data for.

Diabetes

Type 1 or 2, with or without insulin, often stays on simplified issue if there are no severe complications.

Heart disease

An old heart attack often stays simplified. Heart failure or a recent event changes the path.

High blood pressure

It is one of the most common conditions. By itself it almost never pushes you to guaranteed acceptance.

COPD

Many plans still quote. Tobacco and oxygen prescribed for the lungs usually narrow the options.

Cancer

Active treatment often goes to GIWL. Cancer-free and off treatment for a time can return to simplified issue.

Kidney

Stage and whether dialysis is in use matter more than the word “kidney.”

Disability

A Social Security check is not the same as a “no” on the questionnaire. A wheelchair and help with bathing are reviewed separately.

HIV

On appointed simplified-issue companies this is usually a decline. The usual path is GIWL.

Stroke or TIA

An event years ago is not the same as one in the last two years.

When simplified issue often cannot issue

At appointed companies, a plan with questions often cannot issue if one of these situations applies. That is when we look at GIWL. This is not a diagnosis and not a legal list — a quote confirms the product.

  • HIV or AIDS
  • Alzheimer’s or dementia
  • Hospital, nursing home, hospice, skilled nursing, or home health care
  • Oxygen for a lung condition (not sleep apnea)
  • Wheelchair, scooter, or bedridden from illness (not a short-term injury)
  • Cancer in active treatment (some skin cancers or very early stages may still go simplified)
  • Alcohol or drug abuse, or treatment for it, in the last 24 months
  • Mental incapacity or terminal illness
  • Dialysis, end-stage kidney disease, or an organ transplant

COPD with tobacco is another common example: appointed simplified issue often declines, and GIWL may still be a path. Transamerica’s single-condition chart can still consider COPD, diabetes, or kidney disease at Standard when it is the only listed factor. The full profile — build, prescriptions, other diagnoses — decides.

How to apply without wrecking a future claim

The company reviews what you say and what is already in prescription and claims databases. Tell the truth even if you fear a “yes.” A wrong “no” is worse than a plan with a wait.

Frequently asked questions

Does a pre-existing condition stop me from buying final expense?

Not by itself. Most final expense plans exist for older adults with a medical history. What changes is whether the benefit is immediate, graded, or has a two-year wait.

Can I buy an online plan with “no exam and no waiting period”?

At the companies we quote, “no exam” still has questions. Guaranteed acceptance has no questions and always has a wait for natural death. We do not quote a product that combines both.

Do diabetes or high blood pressure send me to guaranteed acceptance?

Almost never, if that is the only condition and there are no severe complications. Quote a level plan first.

Does the price go up because I have a condition?

On a level plan, price mainly follows age, sex, and tobacco, plus class (Preferred or Standard). On GIWL, health does not change the premium — that is why it usually costs more.

Do I have to list every medication?

Yes. Companies review prescription history. A cancer, oxygen, or dialysis drug that does not match your answers can stall or void the contract.

Until what age can I buy?

It depends on the product. Many final expense plans go through 85. Accendo Level can issue through 89. Appointed GIWL usually stops at 80.
