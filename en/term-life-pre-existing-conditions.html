Can you buy term life insurance if you already have a health condition?

Often yes, at another price or a different amount. Term life insurance covers a set number of years: if you die during that period and premiums are current, the person you named receives the amount. A condition that already existed when you apply is not an automatic no. There is also no term policy with no health questions.

The question How it works Paths Questions What matters Cost

Three facts to start with

  • Term covers a need with a date — a mortgage, years of income, debts — not a funeral forever. The low price per dollar is why companies ask more than they do on a small burial plan.
  • The diagnosis word does not decide alone. The date, the treatment, tobacco, weight, and a second problem matter. An old, stable event is not read the same as a recent one or one with complications.
  • If term cannot issue, the next product is not “the same term with no questions.” It is a smaller permanent plan: final expense or, if that questionnaire also cannot issue, guaranteed acceptance (no health questions and about a two-year wait for natural death).

Give the diagnosis, the year, the medications, and whether you use tobacco. That decides the product and the price — not the “no exam” headline.

The question people actually bring

Families shop term when income or a debt still has a date: years left on a mortgage, years until children no longer depend on that paycheck. The amount is usually much larger than a burial plan. So the fear with a diagnosis is concrete: “Can I still protect those years, or will I only be sold a small plan?”

The rest of this page explains what term is and how a company reviews health. Company names come later.

What term is, and what counts as a pre-existing condition

You pay a premium — the regular bill. If you die while the contract is current and inside the term, the beneficiary (the person you named) receives the death benefit: the contract amount. When the term ends, that coverage ends. It is not a savings account. The NAIC describes term this way: it covers one or more years and generally does not build cash value.

On the usual term at these companies, the premium is level: it does not rise just because you have a birthday during the original term, if you pay on time. Typical lengths are 10, 15, 20, 25, or 30 years. Amounts on a full quote often start at $100,000.

A pre-existing condition is a diagnosis, treatment, or symptoms that already existed when you apply. The CDC notes that many people live for years with a chronic disease. That is health. An insurer does not treat the disease. It decides whether that history, as written and as it appears in prescriptions, fits a product it is willing to issue.

How an insurer reviews health on term life

There is no “automatic yes” term. There are always questions. On larger amounts, the company often wants more detail — prescriptions already filled, height and weight, tobacco, and sometimes labs or a nurse visit. A “no” that should have been “yes” can stall or affect a claim. The NAIC reminds consumers that honest answers matter at claim time.

Term sells a large benefit for a relatively small bill. That is why companies are usually stricter than they are on a $10,000 or $15,000 burial plan. A controlled diagnosis can still issue. A recent event, several conditions together, or active treatment can raise the price, lower the amount, or close this product.

There are two real paths, and neither is “no history.” One asks for more detail and sometimes labs; it usually opens the better price per dollar. The other uses a shorter questionnaire and no in-office exam; it usually costs more per dollar and covers lower amounts. If neither can issue, we look at a different permanent product — we do not invent a no-questions term.

Three paths, in plain language

A fuller health review

More questions; depending on age and amount, labs or a visit outside your doctor’s office. Usually the better price per dollar and amounts from $100,000 up, sometimes into the millions on a typical quote. At one appointed company, the lowest advertised price asks, among other things, for no coronary artery disease, no diabetes, and no cancer (except some skin cancers). That is not a promise everyone pays that row.

A shorter questionnaire, no in-office exam

There are still questions and a prescription check. It is faster. American Amicable Easy Term: 10-, 20-, and 30-year terms; $25,000 minimum; $500,000 cap through age 45 and $300,000 from age 46; issue ages 18–70 on 10-year and 18–55 on 30-year. Mutual of Omaha Term Life Express: from $25,000; caps $550,000 at ages 18–50, $450,000 at 51–60, and $350,000 at 61–75. Those products do not offer the lowest advertised price row; they usually have only typical price classes (tobacco or non-tobacco).

When term cannot issue

There is no guaranteed-acceptance term. Guaranteed acceptance is a different product: guaranteed-issue whole life (GIWL). It asks no health questions. The one we quote covers $5,000–$25,000 at ages 50–80 and waits about two years for natural death. If the need is a funeral, not years of income, start with final expense.

No appointed company we quote offers zero questions and a large term benefit from day one. If an ad combines both, it is not a product we quote.

What helps, and what does not

Answering with facts is usually the advantage: you may open a larger amount and a lower price per dollar than a burial plan. The limitation is the same questionnaire: a recent event, active treatment, several conditions together, or tobacco combined with diabetes can close term. Waiting “until I am healthier” only raises the age if the diagnosis is already stable. Waiting can matter if there has just been a heart attack, a stroke, or cancer in treatment: some questions look at time windows.

Can you later switch to a policy that does not expire?

Some term contracts allow conversion: moving from that term policy to a permanent policy at the same company, using the health answers you already gave, if you do it inside the contract window. You pay the permanent price at the age you convert, not the old term price.

That does not replace applying for term today with a real history. The window, the destination products, and the caps are in that policy. Appointed examples: Transamerica Trendsetter Super and Corebridge Select-a-Term. Not every contract converts the same way.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy term if I already have a diagnosis?

Often yes, at another price or a smaller amount. There is no no-questions term. Quote with the real date, medications, and tobacco.

Is there guaranteed-acceptance term?

Not at the companies we quote. Guaranteed acceptance is a small permanent product with a wait for natural death. See the guaranteed-acceptance guide.

Does diabetes get me the lowest internet rate?

Almost never. At one appointed company, that row asks among other things for no diabetes. On Mutual of Omaha’s shorter-questionnaire term, diabetes after age 45 with tobacco or with complications typically will not issue.

I had a heart attack years ago. Is term still open?

It can be, if it is old and there is no heart failure. A recent event or a recent stent changes the file. See also the heart guide if the need is burial, not income.

Do I have to do a blood test?

On Easy Term and Term Life Express there is no in-office lab visit. On a fuller review, depending on age and amount, there may be labs. On Corebridge Select-a-Term, if what you already have in force at that company plus what you apply for exceeds $1,000,000, an exam is usually required.

Term could not issue. Is insurance over?

No. The next step is final expense with questions, or guaranteed acceptance if those cannot issue either. Those are different products, not “the same term cheaper.”

Does HIV open a fixed-term plan?

Not on what we quote for AIDS. Term Life Express excludes medications in that line. The usual path is guaranteed acceptance. See the HIV page.

Does tobacco only raise the price, or can it close the product?

Tobacco is usually a higher price class, not a close by itself. Combined — diabetes after 45 and nicotine on Term Life Express, or COPD and tobacco — it can stop issue. Say nicotine in the last 12 months.

What can change a term application

Each card starts with the health idea in plain language, then lists only what we can verify at appointed companies. Those notes assume that condition is the only factor. Weight, tobacco, a second diagnosis, or several conditions together can still change the price or stop issue.

High blood pressure

In plain language

High blood pressure is common. Controlled with pills, many people work and live with it for years.

What we can verify

  • Alone, term often stays open on a fuller review.
  • The lowest advertised price at one appointed company asks, among other things, for tight control and no coronary disease, diabetes, or cancer.
  • Say whether it is controlled and which medications you take.

Diabetes

In plain language

The body does not use blood sugar well. Type 1 and type 2 are different diseases; both may be treated with food, pills, or insulin.

What we can verify

  • It almost never pays the cheapest internet row: that row, at one appointed company, asks among other things for no diabetes.
  • On Mutual of Omaha’s shorter-questionnaire term, diabetes after age 45 plus tobacco, a higher weight range, or circulation complications typically will not issue.
  • A burial plan with questions is a different product; see the diabetes guide if the need is a funeral, not income.

We do not publish one Transamerica price label for “diabetes alone”: that chart is for agents, and the live offer still depends on date, control, and the rest of the file.

Heart attack or heart disease

In plain language

A heart attack is damage to the heart muscle from a lack of blood. An event ten years ago is not the same as one this year.

What we can verify

  • An old heart attack, without heart failure, can still be term in Transamerica’s best case — usually not the lowest advertised price.
  • Heart failure (the heart does not pump well) on that same chart typically stops that product from issuing.
  • A recent stent or bypass changes the file. Give the year.

COPD or emphysema

In plain language

COPD is a long-term lung disease. Emphysema is a common type. Oxygen at home is not the same as an inhaler.

What we can verify

  • In Transamerica’s best case, COPD or emphysema can still be term, not the cheapest row.
  • Oxygen for a lung condition narrows or closes other products; say so.
  • Tobacco together with COPD can change the product more than the word “COPD” alone.

Cancer

In plain language

Cancer in active treatment is not the same as cancer treated years ago. Some skin cancers are read differently from an internal-organ cancer.

What we can verify

  • Cancer in active treatment: that Transamerica term product typically will not issue.
  • A treated history can still quote; the type and the date matter.
  • The lowest advertised price on Term Life Answers asks, among other things, for no cancer except some skin cancers.

Stroke or TIA

In plain language

A stroke interrupts blood to the brain. A TIA is a brief warning. What remained — speech, walking, memory — matters as much as the date.

What we can verify

  • An old event is not the same as a recent one.
  • On Transamerica, a stroke as the only factor can still quote term in the best case, not at the lowest internet rate.
  • Give the year and whether a limitation remained.

Dialysis or kidney failure

In plain language

Dialysis replaces the work of the kidneys when they no longer filter well. It is not the same as a stone or one kidney.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica, kidney failure or dialysis typically stops that term product from issuing.
  • Then look at final expense or guaranteed acceptance, not large term.
  • See also the kidney disease guide if the need is burial.

HIV or AIDS

In plain language

HIV is the virus. AIDS is the later stage. Antiviral medications show up in the prescriptions companies can already see.

What we can verify

  • We do not quote an appointed fixed-term plan for an AIDS diagnosis.
  • On Term Life Express, certain antivirals in that line are on the list that stops issue.
  • The usual path we quote is guaranteed acceptance, if age and amount fit. See the HIV guide.

We do not have a public chart from another appointed company that issues fixed-term coverage for AIDS. We will not invent a “yes” for a well-controlled HIV file on a product we do not quote.
