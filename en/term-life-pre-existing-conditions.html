Term life insurance if you already have a health condition

Term covers a set number of years: a mortgage, income, debts with a date. A pre-existing condition is not an automatic no on that product. There is also no guaranteed-acceptance term. There are questions, often prescriptions, and on larger amounts, labs. The tables you see online are usually Preferred Best: that class needs a clean history.

Types By condition When not Cost Companies Questions

Three facts to start with

  • There are two term paths at the companies we quote: fully underwritten (better price per dollar, more questions) and simplified issue (Easy Term or Term Life Express: a questionnaire, no in-office exam, usually more per dollar).
  • On Trendsetter Super, high blood pressure can reach Preferred as a best case. Diabetes, a heart attack, COPD, or a stroke usually land at Standard, not Preferred Plus. AIDS, cancer in treatment, dialysis, and congestive heart failure are Decline on that chart.
  • If term will not issue, the other product is not “the same term with no questions.” It is final expense or, if that questionnaire also fails, guaranteed acceptance — smaller amounts and, on GIWL, a two-year wait for natural death.

Give the diagnosis, the date, the medications, and whether you use tobacco. That decides class, amount, and whether the product is still term — not the “no exam” headline.

Three paths, not one ad

The brand name changes. What matters is how many questions there are and which price class can open.

Fully underwritten

Trendsetter Super, Term Life Answers, Select-a-Term, and Assurity Term Life. More detail; sometimes labs or a paramedical visit. Usually the best price per dollar and amounts from $100,000 up. Preferred Plus at Mutual of Omaha asks, among other things, for no coronary disease, no diabetes, and no cancer (except basal or superficial squamous skin).

Simplified issue

Easy Term (American Amicable): 10-, 20-, and 30-year terms; $25,000 minimum; $300,000 cap after age 45. Term Life Express: from $25,000; caps $550,000 at 18–50, $450,000 at 51–60, $350,000 at 61–75; Standard non-tobacco or tobacco only — no Preferred. There are still questions. It is not guaranteed acceptance.

When term will not issue

We do not invent a “no-history term.” The next product is a smaller permanent plan: final expense with questions, or GIWL if those questions cannot issue either.

There is no guaranteed-acceptance term

Corebridge GIWL asks no health questions and covers $5,000–$25,000 at ages 50–80, with a two-year wait for natural death. That is not $250,000 of term. If an ad promises “no questions and a large benefit from day one,” it is not a product we quote.

How we quote term with a history

There is no “term company for people with diabetes” that always wins. There is a questionnaire that fits or does not.

  1. Health facts and money facts. Age, tobacco, height, weight, medications, diagnosis dates, and the term and amount the family still needs. A $10,000 burial plan does not answer the same question as $250,000 for 20 years.
  2. Fully underwritten first if the file can open it. If the history is controlled high blood pressure or an old event without complications, fully underwritten is usually the first try: better price per dollar. Corebridge can use Agile Underwriting+ (ages 20–59, up to $1,000,000) and require labs if in-force plus applied-for exceeds $1,000,000.
  3. Simplified if you do not want labs or the file is tighter. Easy Term or Term Life Express. Faster. On Express, several conditions together above Table 4 decline. A drug on the exclusion list (for example HIV antivirals or certain cancer or dementia drugs) stops Express.

When appointed term is usually not the product

These situations, in the material we use, usually move the file off large term. Then we look at final expense or GIWL. Not a complete legal list.

  • AIDS (Decline on Trendsetter); HIV antivirals on the Term Life Express exclusion list
  • Cancer in active treatment
  • Dialysis or kidney failure
  • Congestive heart failure
  • Terminal illness
  • Diabetes with complications, or diabetes after 45 with tobacco or Table 2 build, on Term Life Express
  • Several conditions that together would exceed Table 4 on Express

A term decline does not erase final expense. Those are different contracts, with different amounts and questions.

What the best-class tables cost (not the price of a diagnosis)

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy term if I already have a diagnosis?

Often yes, at another price class or a smaller amount. There is no no-questions term. Quote with the real date, medications, and tobacco.

Is there guaranteed-acceptance term?

Not at the companies we quote. Guaranteed acceptance is a small permanent product with a wait for natural death. See the guaranteed-acceptance guide.

Does diabetes get me the lowest internet rate?

Almost never. Preferred Plus on Term Life Answers asks, among other things, for no diabetes. On Trendsetter, diabetes as the only factor is usually Standard as a best case. On Term Life Express, diabetes with tobacco or with complications usually declines.

I had a heart attack years ago. Is term still open?

It can be, at Standard on the Trendsetter chart if that is the only factor. CHF is Decline. A recent event, a recent stent, or heart failure change the file. See also the heart (final expense) guide.

Do I have to do a blood test?

On Easy Term and Term Life Express, there is no in-office lab visit. On fully underwritten, depending on age and amount, there may be labs. Select-a-Term requires an exam if Corebridge in-force plus applied-for exceeds $1,000,000.

Term declined me. Is insurance over?

No. The next step is final expense with questions, or GIWL if those cannot issue either. Those are different products, not “the same term cheaper.”

Does HIV open a level term plan?

Not on what we quote. Trendsetter lists AIDS as Decline. Term Life Express excludes medications in that line. The usual path is GIWL. See the HIV page.

Does tobacco only raise the price, or can it close the product?

Tobacco is usually a higher class, not a close by itself. Combined — diabetes after 45 and nicotine on Express, or COPD and tobacco — it can decline. Say nicotine in the last 12 months.
