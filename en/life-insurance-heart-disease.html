Final expense insurance if you have heart disease

Families look for this coverage because a funeral and small debts can fall on relatives. A cardiac history does not close that door by itself. First understand how the insurance works; then what appointed companies publish about heart attack, heart failure, surgery, or devices.

The question How it works Plan types What matters Companies Cost Questions

Three facts to start with

  • “Heart disease” is not one checkbox. Heart attack, coronary disease, heart failure, arrhythmia, stent, or pacemaker can be treated differently on an application.
  • For final expense the usual path is simplified issue: health questions and a prescription check, no office exam. If those answers fit, the plan can be level (full amount from the first covered payment).
  • If the health-questionnaire plan cannot issue, guaranteed-issue whole life (GIWL) has no health questions. For natural death, the full benefit usually does not apply until after about two years. That is a backup option—not where most families start when a heart problem is old and stable.

The sample premiums below are for a level plan if the questionnaire issues. There is no separate “heart rate”: price still follows age, sex, and tobacco. Not an offer.

The question people bring

The CDC places heart disease among the most common causes of death in the United States. That explains the fear: “Will they only offer a plan that waits two years?”

Final expense is permanent life insurance in a small amount, meant for funeral, cemetery, and small debts — not a large income-replacement policy. Medicare does not pay that bill. The rest of this page explains how the underwriting decision is made before looking at company cards.

What “heart” means in health language

The CDC describes coronary artery disease, heart attack, heart failure, and other muscle or valve problems. The NHLBI explains a heart attack as damage to the muscle when blood flow is cut off.

On an application those ideas mix with the cardiologist’s words: angina, angioplasty, atrial fibrillation, bypass, stent, pacemaker, defibrillator. You do not have to translate codes. Say what the doctor wrote and the year. The company matches prescriptions with your answers.

How life insurance reviews a cardiac history

For final expense the usual path is simplified issue: no lab appointment, but there are questions. The company also looks at medications you already filled. A “no” that should have been “yes” can stall or void a claim. The NAIC reminds consumers that honest answers matter when a claim arrives.

What most often moves the product is not the word “heart” alone, but when the event happened, whether there was surgery or a device, whether there is heart failure, and what else is in the file (tobacco, oxygen, another diagnosis, build).

Fully underwritten term is a different product: higher amounts, more detail, sometimes labs. If the need is income or a mortgage — not burial — see the guide to term life with pre-existing conditions.

Three plan types, in plain language

Level or immediate

There are questions. If the company issues, the full amount can apply from the first covered payment. Usually the lowest price per dollar of these three.

Graded or modified

There are still questions. In the early years, a non-accidental death may pay only part of the face amount or return premiums plus the contract’s interest. Each company writes that period differently.

Guaranteed acceptance

No health questions. Within age and amount, your medical history does not decline you. There is always about a two-year wait for non-accidental death. That product is called GIWL: guaranteed-issue whole life. The one we quote is ages 50–80 and $5,000–$25,000.

No appointed company we quote offers zero questions and a full natural-death benefit from day one. Ads that mix those two ideas almost always still have a questionnaire.

What helps, and what does not

Answering the questionnaire is usually the advantage: it can open more face amount, a lower price, and no two-year wait. The limit is that the same questionnaire can send you to a more expensive class, a graded plan, or GIWL if the event is recent, there is heart failure, a device, tobacco, or other diagnoses. Waiting to apply “when you are healthier” only raises the age if the history has already been stable for years.

What can change an application with a cardiac history

Each card starts with the idea in plain language, then lists only what we can verify in appointed-company materials. Those notes usually assume a single factor. Build, tobacco, a second diagnosis, or several listed conditions together can still change the class or decline.

When a company names a class, it is a price bucket on that one product if that health item is the only factor they are scoring. On Transamerica FE Express, Premier is usually the better price and Select costs more. On Immediate Solution, Preferred is usually the better price and Standard costs more. Graded waits in the early years. Decline means that product will not issue.

For review: we do not publish a single “two-year” cutoff that applies to every appointed company at once. In Mejor Vida Insurance’s internal flow we ask about heart attack, stroke, or TIA in the last two years because that window changes many applications — not because it is an identical published rule on Living Promise, Accendo, Transamerica, and Americo.

Heart attack (single event)

In plain language

A heart attack damages the heart muscle when blood flow is cut off. The NHLBI describes it as a medical emergency. For insurance, the date matters as much as the diagnosis.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica’s FE Express product, one heart attack less than a year ago typically goes in Select (a higher monthly price than Premier). After a year, that same chart typically uses Premier. That assumes the heart attack is the only health item they are scoring.
  • Transamerica also sells Immediate Solution. Those two products use different charts and different class names. A result on one is not a result on the other.
  • Accendo Preferred uses different clocks: a heart attack in the last year typically goes to the Modified plan; one to two years ago typically goes to Standard Level (full amount from day one, higher price than Preferred); older than two years can still be Preferred Level if every other question is no.
  • Living Promise does not use that one-year chart. It uses yes/no questions. An older, stable heart attack can still be a level plan if every question is no — we do not tell every family that every company will offer day-one coverage after eight years.

Multiple heart attacks

In plain language

More than one event usually means more medical risk and more questions on the application.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica FE Express, more than one heart attack typically goes in Select on that chart — not an automatic “no.”
  • Accendo does not have a separate “more than one heart attack” line. It uses the date of diagnosis or treatment: last year, last two years, or older.
  • Mutual of Omaha Living Promise does not use that Select/Premier chart. It uses yes/no health questions: all no can be a level plan; some yes answers mean graded only; others mean this application cannot issue.

Heart failure

In plain language

Not the same as a heart attack. The heart does not pump with its usual strength. Congestive and diastolic are forms physicians name.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica Immediate Solution, “heart disease” can be in the better class (Preferred). Heart failure is treated separately and can land in Standard — that is still an offer on that summary, not an automatic no.
  • On Transamerica FE Express, congestive heart failure typically goes in Select.
  • On Accendo, congestive heart failure on this application typically means this Accendo application cannot issue. Some heart-failure medicines (for example Entresto) are also flagged on Accendo’s medication list for Preferred, Standard, and Modified. Quote with the diagnosis and the medicines.

Coronary disease, angina, arrhythmia

In plain language

Narrowed arteries, cardiac chest pain, or irregular rhythm can exist without a documented heart attack.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica FE Express, coronary disease without a heart attack or surgery, angina, irregular rhythm, and atrial fibrillation typically go in Premier if they happened in the last five years.
  • Immediate Solution groups many of these under “heart disease” as Preferred in its summary. We do not reprint every medical row here.
  • On Accendo, angina in the last year typically goes to Modified; angina one to two years ago typically goes to Standard Level. Accendo does not use Transamerica’s five-year Premier row.

Stent, bypass, angioplasty, pacemaker

In plain language

These are procedures or devices to improve flow or rhythm. The year of surgery usually matters more than the device name.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica FE Express, a pacemaker, stent, valve surgery, bypass, or angioplasty less than a year ago typically goes in Select. After one year, that chart typically uses Premier.
  • Immediate Solution does not treat a pacemaker as its own automatic “no” in the company summary we have. It points back to the heart-disease rules.
  • Accendo does not name stent, bypass, or pacemaker as their own rows. They count as heart or circulatory surgery. Last year typically goes to Modified; one to two years ago typically Standard Level; older than two years can still be Preferred Level if every other question is no.

Implantable defibrillator (ICD)

In plain language

Not a simple pacemaker. It detects dangerous arrhythmias and can deliver a shock.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica FE Express, a cardiac defibrillator (ICD) is typically a decline on that product.
  • Immediate Solution does not list the implant as an automatic decline in the company summary we have. It says to follow the heart-disease rules. We will not promise Preferred or decline without the rest of your history.
  • Accendo does not name an ICD as its own line. The implant is treated as heart or circulatory surgery by date. If heart failure is also on the record, this Accendo application typically cannot issue.
  • These two Transamerica products do not treat a defibrillator the same way. Accendo is a third set of rules. Ask which product is being quoted. Do not assume every company says yes, or that every company says no.

Tobacco after a cardiac event

In plain language

Smoking or vaping nicotine usually raises the premium. That is not the same as an automatic decline.

What we can verify

  • On Americo Eagle Select, smoking plus heart disease, diabetes, or a lung disease typically uses a higher smoker price class (called ES2) — not necessarily a decline.
  • Sample prices on this page assume no tobacco. Tobacco in the last 12 months usually uses a higher price.

Hospice, hospital now, pending surgery

In plain language

If you are in very intense active treatment or cannot leave the hospital, simplified issue is usually not the right product right now.

What we can verify

  • On Transamerica FE Express, current hospice or palliative care is typically a decline. So is being hospitalized two or more times in the past year (other than childbirth).
  • Guaranteed-issue whole life may still be available if age and amount fit. That plan waits about two years for a natural-death benefit.

These notes are not a quote. The live application still decides.

Mutual of Omaha Living Promise

Living Promise is a different kind of decision. It does not use Premier, Select, Preferred, or Standard classes for a heart history. It uses yes/no health questions.

  • If every health question is no, the person is eligible for the level plan — full amount from the first covered payment, if the company issues.
  • A yes on some questions means graded only — in the early years a non-accidental death pays less than the face amount.
  • A yes on other questions means this Living Promise application may not issue.
  • Heart failure, heart attack, heart surgery, irregular rhythm, a pacemaker, or a defibrillator can still change a Living Promise decision — including a decline. That is not Transamerica’s one-year Select/Premier chart. Quote with the real dates.

Accendo Preferred

Accendo Final Expense is a different kind of decision from Transamerica. It does not use Premier or Select. It uses yes/no health questions, then a prescription check.

  • Congestive heart failure on this Accendo application typically means this application cannot issue.
  • A heart attack, angina, cardiomyopathy, or heart or circulatory surgery (including stent, bypass, or pacemaker) in the last year typically goes to the Modified plan — full amount right away only for accidental death; non-accidental death in the early years pays less than the face amount.
  • The same heart items one to two years ago typically go to Standard Level — full amount from day one if issued, at a higher price than Preferred.
  • Older than two years, and every other health question no, can still be Preferred Level.
  • Some heart-failure medicines (for example Entresto) are flagged on all three Accendo plans. The live application and the prescription check still decide.

That is why we do not tell every family that every company waits two years, or that every company pays from day one.

On Americo Eagle Select, heart disease by itself is not an automatic no. If you have two of heart disease, diabetes, or stroke (and you do not use nicotine), they usually use a higher price class called ES2. If you use nicotine and also have heart disease, diabetes, or a lung disease, they use the ES2 smoker class.

Frequently asked questions

I had a heart attack eight years ago. Is there a two-year wait?

Not necessarily. Many simplified plans still issue level if the rest of the questionnaire is clean. The recent-time window is what most often changes the answer. We do not promise the same result at every company.

Does a stent or bypass block an immediate plan?

On Transamerica FE Express those procedures have time windows (Select or Premier by years). On Immediate Solution they refer to the heart-disease rules. Other companies ask for the year. Give the exact date.

Is heart failure the same as a heart attack?

No. The CDC and physicians separate them. In the Immediate Solution summary, “heart disease” can be Preferred and heart failure Standard; on FE Express heart failure is Select. Use your cardiologist’s words.

Does a defibrillator close insurance?

It depends on the product. FE Express lists it as Decline. Immediate Solution refers to Heart Disease. Do not say “always” or “never” without naming the product. GIWL may still be open.

Can I own a parent’s policy if they have heart problems?

Yes, if there is insurable interest and the parent signs. You do not answer their history for them. See the family guides.

Does tobacco after a heart attack matter?

Yes. Tobacco in the last 12 months is usually a tobacco rate. On Eagle Select, nicotine with heart, diabetes, or respiratory disease pushes to ES2 smoker.

Should I wait until I am “healthier” to apply?

If the event was recent, it can be worth waiting out the window the company asks about. If it was years ago and you are stable, waiting only raises the age. Quote now with real facts.
