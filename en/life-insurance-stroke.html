Final expense insurance if you had a stroke or TIA

The CDC explains that a stroke happens when blood flow to the brain is cut off; a TIA is a short episode with similar symptoms that resolve. For final expense, the year of the event matters more than the label. A 2015 stroke, with recovery and no wheelchair from illness, often stays on simplified issue. An event in the last two years is asked separately.

What it is Underwriting What changes Cost Companies Questions

Three facts to start with

  • On Transamerica’s chart, stroke and TIA are Standard; with an activity credit they can move to Preferred if that is the only factor.
  • In Mejor Vida Insurance’s flow we ask about heart attack, stroke, or TIA in the last two years. That window narrows Living Promise, Accendo, and other simplified plans more than an old episode.
  • If the stroke left a wheelchair from illness, dementia, or facility care, the file is no longer “stroke only.” See disability and the hub knockout list.

Give the date, whether it was a stroke or a TIA, and whether you were left needing help to walk or with memory problems. Do not round “a couple of years ago” if it was eleven months ago.

Stroke and TIA, in one sentence each

The CDC: a stroke damages the brain from a clot or a bleed. A TIA is a brief warning. Both are treated as neurologic events on an application, not as “dizziness.”

High blood pressure and tobacco are factors the NHLBI highlights. If you also have blood pressure or heart disease, say so: the questionnaire is no longer a single impairment.

How appointed companies look at it

Living Promise and Accendo ask about hospital stays and recent events; each form uses its own time window. Transamerica publishes Standard for stroke and TIA, with possible Preferred if there is an activity credit. Hospitalization in 12 months is Standard; current, Decline. That stacks with the stroke; it does not replace it.

A level plan, if you qualify, can pay from day one. GIWL comes in when simplified issue cannot issue — for example a very recent event plus a wheelchair from illness or dementia.

What usually changes the answer

The date and the aftermath weigh more than the word “stroke.”

  • Event in the last two years: it has to be dated. Many simplified plans narrow.
  • Chair, facility, or memory loss after the event: see disability and the GIWL list (dementia, facility, wheelchair from illness).
  • Pending surgery, hospital now, or hospice: Decline at Transamerica and on our simplified list.
  • High blood pressure only, no stroke: that is a different page. Do not mix a TIA with “my pressure spiked one day.”

Frequently asked questions

I had a TIA five years ago. Is there a wait?

Often not, if the rest of the questionnaire is clean. At Transamerica a TIA is Standard (Preferred with an activity credit). Quote with the real year.

The stroke was 14 months ago. Which product?

That is inside the two-year window we use. Simplified issue may narrow. Have dates ready and ask; do not answer “no” to “in the last two years?”

I have a weak side but I walk. Does that count as a wheelchair?

If you do not use a chair or scooter from illness, describe it that way. If you do use a chair, see the disability guide: Living Promise and Accendo often treat it differently from Transamerica.

Does tobacco after a stroke decline me?

It is usually a tobacco rate, not an automatic close, if the rest passes. Within 12 months of nicotine, Transamerica applies a tobacco rate.

Is GIWL automatic after a stroke?

No. An old stroke without serious aftermath often stays on simplified issue. GIWL is for when the questionnaire cannot issue.

Can I buy for a parent who had a stroke?

Yes, if there is insurable interest and the parent can sign and answer. A power of attorney does not replace that signature. See the family guides.
