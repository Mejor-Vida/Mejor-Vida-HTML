What is guaranteed acceptance life insurance and how does it work?

Guaranteed acceptance is a small whole life policy with no medical exam and no health questions. If you meet the product’s age and amount rules, you are not turned down for medical history. In exchange there is a typical two-year wait for natural death, and you usually pay more per dollar of coverage.

Key points

  • These policies always have a waiting period (in practice, two years) for a non-accidental death.
  • For some serious health issues it is the only way to buy a new policy. Most seniors with diabetes, high blood pressure, or other common conditions can still qualify for a level plan with health questions — usually cheaper, and without that two-year wait.
  • Mejor Vida Insurance quotes Corebridge GIWL as guaranteed-issue whole life: issue ages 50–80 and amounts from $5,000 to $25,000.

Start with a final-expense plan that uses health questions. Guaranteed acceptance is the backup when that questionnaire cannot issue an immediate benefit.

How it works

There is no in-office exam, no bloodwork, and no health or lifestyle questionnaire. Within the product’s age and amount, the company does not decline you for pre-existing conditions. That is why it is called guaranteed acceptance (or guaranteed issue).

On the products Mejor Vida Insurance quotes, this is whole life, not term or universal life. You may also hear “graded” or “modified” benefit — the idea is the same. We do not quote guaranteed acceptance on term life.

Premiums stay level if you keep the policy in force. On Corebridge GIWL, required payments stop at or before age 90, per the contract. The face amount cannot be raised or lowered after issue.

What happens during the waiting period

If death is not a covered accident in the first two years, the family does not receive the full face amount. On Corebridge GIWL that limited benefit is 110% of premiums paid. From year three on, the full face applies for a covered natural death. A covered accident can pay the face from day one (with contract exclusions). For suicide, the contract typically refunds premiums during the stated period.

No company we quote offers guaranteed acceptance and a full natural-death benefit from day one. Ads that say “no exam and no waiting period” almost always still have health questions.

Do you actually need guaranteed acceptance?

Many seniors assume it is their only option because they have diabetes, high blood pressure, COPD, a prior heart attack, or other conditions. On simplified final expense those situations often still qualify for a level or immediate plan: there are questions, not an exam, and the full benefit can apply from the first covered payment. That path is usually cheaper. Guaranteed acceptance fits when the questionnaire cannot issue a level plan — not as the first try.

When guaranteed acceptance is often the only way to get a new policy

At appointed companies, a simplified-issue plan (with questions) often cannot issue if one of these situations applies. That is when we look at guaranteed acceptance. This is not a diagnosis and not a legal list — a quote confirms the product.

  • HIV or AIDS
  • Alzheimer’s or dementia
  • Currently in a hospital, nursing home, hospice, skilled nursing facility, or on home health care
  • Oxygen for a lung condition (not sleep apnea)
  • Wheelchair, scooter, or bedridden because of an illness (not a short-term injury)
  • Active cancer treatment (some skin cancers or very early stages may still go simplified)
  • Alcohol or drug abuse, or treatment for either, in the last 24 months
  • Mental incapacity
  • Terminal illness
  • Dialysis, end-stage kidney disease, or an organ transplant

COPD with tobacco is another common example: appointed simplified issue often declines, and GIWL may still be a path. If none of these apply, the usual first step is a level plan.

Guaranteed acceptance vs. simplified issue (“no-exam”)

“No medical exam” is not the same as “no health questions.” Ads for “no-exam” life insurance almost always still use a questionnaire. If you are approved on a level plan, there is usually no two-year wait for a covered natural death.

Simplified issue

  • Health questions and database review.
  • You may get immediate, graded, or a decline.
  • If you qualify for level, the full benefit can apply from day one.
  • Usually costs less per dollar of coverage.

Guaranteed acceptance

  • No health questions and no medical tests.
  • Within age and amount, you are not declined for medical history.
  • Always a two-year wait for a non-accidental death.
  • Higher premium; lower amount cap (here, up to $25,000).

How much it costs

Price depends on exact age, sex, and amount. Health history does not change this premium because there are no health questions. Frequently asked questions

Can I be denied guaranteed acceptance because of health?

Not for medical history, if you are inside the product’s age and amount. You still have to meet contract rules (identity, payment, where the product is filed). Corebridge GIWL is not quoted in every state.

Does health change the price?

No. On guaranteed acceptance the premium comes from age, sex, and amount. That is why it can cost more than a level plan: the company cannot select by health.

Is there guaranteed-acceptance term life?

Not on what we quote. These plans are small whole life for final expenses.

I have diabetes or high blood pressure. Is this my only option?

Almost never. Common conditions often still qualify for simplified final expense, with questions and without that two-year wait if the plan is level. Quote that path first.

What does the family receive if I die in year one?

If it is not a covered accident, GIWL pays 110% of premiums paid — not the $10,000 or $25,000 face. A covered accident can pay the face. Suicide follows the contract’s premium-refund rules.

Until what age can I buy it?

Appointed GIWL issues ages 50–80. After 80, Mejor Vida Insurance looks at level final-expense plans (some through 85; Accendo Level through 89), not an automatic yes with no questionnaire.

How much coverage can I buy?

On GIWL, $5,000–$25,000. A level plan at another appointed company can go higher (for example Living Promise Level up to about $50,000) if you qualify.

Does “no exam” mean no questions?

No. No exam is usually simplified issue: there is a questionnaire. No health questions is guaranteed acceptance, with a wait.

Do premiums go up every year?

No, if you keep the policy in force. On GIWL, required payments stop at or before age 90, per the contract.

Can I use the benefit while I am alive?

Some GIWL contracts include chronic- or terminal-illness acceleration, with rules and state variations. That is not a substitute for long-term-care insurance. We confirm it on the quote.
