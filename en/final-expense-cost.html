The Cost of Final Expense Insurance

Final expense (burial) insurance often costs about $30–$100 per month for $10,000 of coverage, depending on age, sex, health, and tobacco use. The tables below show illustrative monthly premiums — not binding quotes.

How insurance companies determine the cost

Your price will not match someone else’s unless their profile is nearly identical. These variables usually matter most. Factors such as race, ethnicity, sexual orientation, marital status, credit score, and education level are not typically used to set premiums.

Gender

Women usually pay less because they live longer on average. In Montana, unisex rates can price women like men.

Policy type

Term usually costs less than whole life because it often ends around age 80. Health-question plans normally cost less than guaranteed acceptance.

Age

The older you are at issue, the higher the price. Whole life premiums don’t rise later; many term burial plans increase every few years.

Health

No-waiting-period plans require health questions. Health issues can raise the price slightly to moderately depending on the company.

Tobacco usage

Cigarette smoking raises the price. Other tobacco (pipe, chew, e-cigarettes, cigars) depends on the carrier.

Death benefit

The more coverage you buy, the higher the price.

Gender

In most states, men pay more (often about 30%) because they live fewer years on average than women. Exception: Montana requires unisex rates on some products, which can raise women’s prices relative to other states.

Age

The older you are at issue, the more coverage usually costs. Many carriers set a distinct rate for each age (51, 52, 53…). A few use age brackets (for example 50–55); that’s less common, but it happens.

Policy type: term vs. whole life

Term life lasts for a set period or until a certain age (sometimes near 80) and may increase every few years. It’s cheaper per dollar of coverage, but when it ends you typically get nothing back and may be uninsured.

Whole life final expense is permanent: coverage does not expire by age and the premium does not increase over time if kept in force. That’s why it usually costs more than a term “burial” plan.

Within whole life final expense there are two common paths:

  • Simplified issue (health questions, no exam). You may qualify for level benefits (often no wait or a short wait) or a graded plan based on health.
  • Guaranteed acceptance. No health questions or exam; you won’t be declined for medical history, but there is almost always a two-year waiting period. Death in that period usually returns premiums plus interest (often ~10%), not the full death benefit.

On price: guaranteed acceptance is usually based on age, sex, and face amount. Simplified issue also factors health and tobacco — and in most cases a level plan with no waiting period costs less than guaranteed issue.

Health (if you want a no-waiting-period plan)

Most carriers group health into 1–3 classes with different prices. Common labels (each company uses them differently):

  • Level / Preferred. Usually the lowest cost and no waiting period; needs moderate to good health.
  • Graded / Standard. Mid-tier; often partial coverage or a wait in the first two years. Some carriers (including Mutual of Omaha Living Promise Graded) use “graded” for a full two-year wait — wording varies.
  • Modified / Basic. Most expensive and almost always a two-year wait; typically tied to more serious health issues.

Health also limits how much you can buy. Example with Mutual of Omaha Living Promise: level often offers $2,000–$50,000; graded often $2,000–$20,000.

Tobacco usage

Cigarettes reduce life expectancy, so smokers pay more. Other forms (pipe, chew, e-cigarettes, cigars) are not always treated the same — it depends on the carrier.

Coverage amount

Age, sex, tobacco, and health usually set your premium per $1,000 of benefit. Then it’s multiplied by the face amount you choose. It’s proportional: a $25,000 policy costs roughly five times as much as a $5,000 policy. The more coverage you buy, the higher the price — Julie will show the exact premium when you quote.

State of residence

Your state determines which companies and products are available. Current licenses are listed on the licenses page. Some carriers do not offer every product in every state.

In Montana, unisex pricing rules on some products mean women often pay more than women in other states, because they’re charged the same rate as men.

What about $9.95/month or “pennies per day” TV prices?

Companies that advertise on TV, online, or by mail often highlight teaser rates that are nowhere near what most people pay. Never trust a paid-ad price alone.

When ads say “starting at $10 a month” or “coverage for pennies a day,” they often quote very little coverage ($500–$1,000) or a young, healthy profile — not the $10,000–$20,000 many families need for a funeral.

Also ignore claims that sound like “free burial insurance for seniors,” “government burial insurance,” or “state-regulated final expense” as if it were a free benefit. None of those is true. To know your real cost, get a quote or use the table on this page.

Typical funeral expenses to consider

Coverage amount drives premium. Estimating funeral (and other end-of-life) costs helps you choose the right face amount.

In the states we currently serve, Funeralocity package averages (Mejor Vida dataset, captured Jul 2026) are roughly:

  • Full-service burial: about $8,200–$8,600 (NE ~$8,620 · KS ~$8,640 · CO ~$8,162 · NV ~$8,538)
  • Full-service cremation: about $5,800–$6,500 (NE ~$6,530 · KS ~$6,452 · CO ~$5,840 · NV ~$6,095)
  • Direct cremation: often much lower (in these states, state averages typically fall between about $1,500 and $3,000)

Sample funeral-home components (NE/KS/CO/NV average, Funeralocity) plus merchandise options from our calculator:

  • Basic funeral home services: ~$2,180
  • Transfer of remains: ~$365
  • Embalming: ~$735
  • Viewing: ~$365
  • Funeral / memorial service: ~$550
  • Casket (Funeralocity median): ~$3,500 · calculator options from ~$995
  • Vault (calculator): ~$995–$2,495
  • Cemetery property (calculator): ~$1,500–$3,500
  • Opening/closing the grave (calculator): ~$1,500–$3,500
  • Flowers (calculator): ~$250–$1,000
  • Crematory fee (FO average): ~$475
  • Urn (calculator): ~$195–$995

There may also be other end-of-life expenses outside the funeral, such as:

  • Mortgage
  • Credit card debt
  • Other outstanding debts
  • Attorney fees
  • Medical bills
  • Automobile loan
  • Other unsecured debt

With those totals, many families plan $5,000–$20,000 of coverage — not $50,000–$100,000 — unless they also want room for debts or other costs. Use our funeral cost calculator for a state- and ceremony-based estimate.

How to find the best price on funeral insurance

The best burial insurance companies usually won’t let you buy online without talking to a licensed agent. Nearly all of them sell through licensed agencies (such as Mejor Vida).

Most plans sold directly by carriers are guaranteed acceptance: often the most expensive option, and they always include a two-year waiting period. No-waiting-period plans that fully insure you from day one are almost exclusively sold through licensed agents.

The most effective way to secure the cheapest final expense insurance is to work with a licensed broker that represents multiple carriers and compares quotes on your behalf.

  • Answer health questions honestly — that often unlocks better pricing than guaranteed acceptance.
  • Compare multiple carriers — each scores health and age differently; none is cheapest for everyone.
  • Use bank draft and ask about semi-annual or annual discounts.
  • Work with an independent agent like Julie at Mejor Vida, who shops several carriers on your behalf.
  • Estimate funeral costs first so you don’t buy too much or too little.

Frequently asked questions

Which company has the cheapest burial insurance?

Usually, Mutual of Omaha is among the lowest premiums for final expense — but no single company is the best burial insurance for everyone, since prices vary by age, health, and product. Compare price and financial strength across the companies you’re considering with Julie.

How much does final expense insurance cost?

Many people pay about $30–$100 per month for around $10,000 of coverage, but age, sex, health, and tobacco change the price. Use this page’s table as a guide, then quote for a personalized number.

Do burial insurance premiums increase as you age?

On most whole life final expense policies, the premium does not increase over time if the policy stays in force. Some term “burial” plans do increase every few years and may end at a set age.

Does it cost more to get funeral insurance without a waiting period?

Not necessarily. A level plan with health questions (no wait or a short wait) usually costs less than guaranteed acceptance, which almost always has a two-year wait.

What are the typical ways companies allow you to make payments?

Monthly bank draft is most common. Some allow card or billed payments; bank draft often avoids fees. Ask whether semi-annual or annual pay has a discount.

Can you make bulk payments on a burial insurance policy for a discount?

Some carriers offer modal discounts (semi-annual/annual). Julie can confirm by product.

What is the difference between final expense insurance and pre-need insurance?

Final expense pays a cash benefit to your beneficiary; a prepaid/pre-need funeral is often tied to a specific funeral home. See our guide on final expense vs. prepaid funeral.

How does final expense insurance work?

You choose a face amount, pay premiums, and at death the insurer pays the benefit to your beneficiary (subject to policy terms, including any waiting period). The money can cover the funeral or other needs.

Do I need a medical exam?

Often no. Most final expense plans use a simplified health questionnaire. Guaranteed acceptance skips exams but usually costs more and includes a wait.

Why do you ask for my state?

Product and form availability depend on where you live and sign. Current licenses are listed on the licenses page.
