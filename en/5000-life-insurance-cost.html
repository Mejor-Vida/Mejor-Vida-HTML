How much a $5,000 life insurance policy costs

A $5,000 policy is usually small whole life for burial or leftover bills — not a large term plan. This page shows monthly rates by age, how appointed companies compare, and whether $5,000 is enough for a funeral.

Updated Aug 2026

Key takeaways

  • A $5,000 policy is a small, permanent amount — usually whole life sold as final-expense or burial coverage, with health questions and no medical exam on most appointed plans.
  • $5,000 can help with a simple cremation, but a traditional burial often costs more (about $8,200–$8,600 on average).

$5,000 guaranteed acceptance rates

Guaranteed-acceptance whole life skips health questions. You generally cannot be turned down for medical history (within the product’s age and amount limits). In exchange, there is almost always a two-year waiting period for natural death — if death happens in that window, the family typically receives premiums paid plus a contract interest amount, not the full $5,000.

A level plan with honest health answers often costs less than guaranteed issue and can start coverage sooner. These figures are the lowest appointed guaranteed-acceptance monthly premiums from appointed companies compared by Mejor Vida Insurance for a $5,000 death benefit.

Is there a $5,000 term life option?

Term life lasts for a set number of years (or until a set age). When the term ends, the coverage ends — there is usually no cash value to take with you.

Appointed term products Mejor Vida Insurance quotes are not issued at $5,000. The smallest published face is $100,000. The chart below is 10-year term at that amount. For a $5,000 need, families almost always use a whole-life / final-expense plan instead.

Is $5,000 enough?

It depends on the kind of service your family wants. Using Funeralocity package averages in Mejor Vida Insurance’s dataset (captured Jul 2026):

  • Direct cremation: often much lower — package averages typically fall between about $1,500 and $3,000
  • Full-service cremation: about $5,800–$6,500
  • Full-service burial: about $8,200–$8,600

So $5,000 can be a meaningful start for cremation or to help with leftover bills. It usually will not cover a traditional burial on its own. If you also want room for debts or travel, many families look at $10,000–$20,000 instead. Use the funeral cost calculator for a state-based estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Will I need a medical exam for $5,000 of coverage?

Usually no. At this amount, appointed final-expense and small whole-life plans typically use a health questionnaire instead of a doctor visit. Guaranteed-acceptance plans skip the health questions but add a two-year wait for natural death.

Is $5,000 enough to pay for a funeral?

It can help with a simple cremation. A traditional burial often costs about $8,200–$8,600, so many families choose a higher amount if burial is the goal.

Should I buy term or whole life for $5,000?

For this small amount, whole life / final expense is the usual path: coverage can last for life and the premium is designed to stay level. Traditional term that Mejor Vida quotes starts at much larger face amounts ($100,000+).

Why do men pay more than women on the chart?

In most states, insurers price men higher because they live fewer years on average. Montana requires unisex rates on some products, which can change women’s prices relative to other states.

Do these premiums go up as I get older?

On most level whole-life final-expense policies, the premium does not increase after issue if you keep the policy in force. Your issue age still matters — applying later usually means a higher starting price.
