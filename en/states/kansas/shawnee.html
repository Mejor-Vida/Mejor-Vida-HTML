What final expense insurance is

It is whole life insurance in a modest amount ($5,000 to $25,000 is typical). The benefit is paid in cash to your beneficiary. It can cover a funeral, cremation, medical bills, and small debts. It is not a prepaid funeral: it is not tied to one funeral home or one plot.

It is also called burial insurance or funeral insurance. That is the same smaller permanent life product; the name does not require spending the check on a specific funeral. What this insurance is.

Four approval paths (and level premiums)

The approval path is not the same as your monthly price. The first three boxes are how the insurer approves the policy; the fourth explains why nearly all use level whole-life premiums. Carriers and age limits differ; a quote confirms what applies to you.

Simplified issue

What it is: health questions, no medical exam. If approved, the full benefit usually pays from day one.

Often the best fit if: you can answer honestly and qualify without a waiting period. Many people from about 50 to 85.

Cost: usually the lowest premium for the same face amount.

Modified issue (graded benefit)

What it is: health questions, but answers do not qualify for immediate full coverage. For the first two or three years, payout is often a percentage of the benefit (sometimes 30%–40% in year one, more in year two) or return of premiums; then 100%.

Often the best fit if: you have moderate health issues—diabetes with complications, COPD, controlled heart failure, etc.—that miss simplified issue but do not need guaranteed acceptance.

Cost: typically between simplified and guaranteed for the same coverage.

Guaranteed acceptance

What it is: no health questions. A two- or three-year waiting period: natural death before that ends pays return of premiums plus interest or a percentage—not the full amount.

Often the best fit if: home oxygen, dialysis, recent cancer, or other answers that close the other paths—or you do not want health questions.

Cost: higher premiums than simplified or modified for the same coverage.

Level premiums (whole life)

What it is: all three paths above are almost always whole life: the payment is set at issue and does not rise as you age when paid on time. It does not expire like term.

Often the best fit if: you live on a fixed income and want a predictable monthly bill for funeral costs and small debts.

Cost: driven by age, sex, smoker status, and approval path. Many families quote before the next birthday.

The burial plot is a separate bill

Funeral-home prices in the table do not include the burial plot (the grave space). If there is a burial, the cemetery sends its own bill.

What the cemetery usually bills

1

The plot (grave space)

The space in the cemetery ground. The cemetery bills it, not the funeral home.

2

Opening and closing

The cemetery opens the grave for burial and closes it afterward. It is almost always a separate charge.

3

Vault

Most cemeteries require a vault for ground burial so the grave does not sink. It is almost never in the funeral-home package.

4

Headstone or marker

The plaque or monument that marks the grave. The cemetery approves size and material and charges to set it.

What else to arrange after a death

The funeral-home package does not include the grave marker, the death certificate copies, or the Social Security paperwork.

Where to buy a headstone or other monument

A headstone, a flush marker, or another monument is bought separately from the funeral and from the plot. The cemetery has to approve the size and material, and it charges to set the stone. The Department of Veterans Affairs may provide a marker if the person was a veteran.

Ask the cemetery offices above for the marker rules and the setting fee. This page does not name a monument company unless that company publishes its own address.

Death certificate

Certified copies of a Kansas death record are issued by the Kansas Department of Health and Environment. The fee and the application are on that page. The funeral home usually files the certificate. Kansas Office of Vital Statistics.

Social Security

The funeral home usually reports the death to Social Security. If it does not, call 1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778). A spouse may get a one-time payment of $255. If there is no spouse, some children may qualify. Monthly survivor benefits are available for certain family members. Social Security does not pay the benefit for the month of death, so that payment has to be returned. What to do when someone dies.

Veterans, the insurance, and the court

  • If the person was a veteran, the Department of Veterans Affairs can help with the burial and with a marker. Start at VA burials and memorials.
  • A life insurance beneficiary files the claim with a certified death certificate. A final expense check is not tied to one funeral home.
  • The probate court in the county where the person lived handles the will and the estate. The bank and that court ask for certified copies.

Shawnee FAQs

Is it the same as burial or funeral insurance?

Yes. Final expense, burial, and funeral insurance usually describe the same smaller permanent life policy. In Shawnee the check is paid in cash to your beneficiary; it is not tied to one funeral home or one plot.

Do you serve Merriam and other towns near Shawnee?

Yes. Official quotes are by phone for Kansas residents in Shawnee, Merriam, De Soto, and Lake Quivira. There is no public walk-in office.

Do I need a medical exam?

Many final expense policies in Kansas use health questions and no exam. If health is a barrier, guaranteed acceptance may be available, usually with a waiting period.

Does the policy pay from day one?

If you are approved for simplified issue, the full benefit typically pays from day one on covered claims. Guaranteed issue pays a limited benefit for the first two or three years, then the full benefit.

How much coverage is usually enough for cremation in Shawnee?

The FCA-GKC 2025 survey has no cremation-with-memorial package, so this table does not name funeral homes. The estimator uses $2,553 as the Kansas average for direct cremation; that figure is not one home’s price. Many families choose $5,000 to $10,000 for the service, urn, travel, and small bills. A traditional burial often needs more: the Kansas average is $8,640, and the plot, vault, and marker are extra.

What is the difference between a prepaid funeral and this insurance?

A prepaid plan is tied to one funeral home or plot and may lock that price. Final expense insurance pays cash to your beneficiary. They can use it at any funeral home in Shawnee, Pleasant View, or for other final bills.

Does the funeral include the cemetery plot?

No. The funeral home price is one bill. The plot is another. Ask Pleasant View or Shawnee Cemetery (913-631-5200) for its list. Resale plots can cost less; the cemetery still has to change the deed.
