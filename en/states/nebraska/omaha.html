What final expense insurance is

It is whole life insurance in a modest amount ($5,000 to $25,000 is typical). The benefit is paid in cash to your beneficiary. It can cover a funeral, cremation, medical bills, and small debts. It is not a prepaid funeral: it is not tied to one funeral home or one plot.

It is also called burial insurance or funeral insurance. That is the same smaller permanent life product; the name does not require spending the check on a specific funeral. What this insurance is.

  • Simplified issue: health questions, no exam. If approved, the full benefit usually pays from day one.
  • Guaranteed acceptance: no health questions, with a two- or three-year waiting period.
  • Level premiums when paid on time; the policy does not expire at a set age the way term does.

What changes from one funeral home to another

The real gap is the service package. A casket is included only when that home’s package says so; the plot never is.

Start with the least expensive package

Direct cremation is the cheapest published shortcut: $1,755 at Braman (crematory extra), $2,795 at Kahler-Dolce, and $2,870 at John A. Gentleman. The estimator uses $2,958 for Nebraska. That number is funeral home expenses only: urn, flowers, and death certificates are extra.

The same package name does not include the same items

Braman’s memorial on its GPL is $3,480. Kahler-Dolce publishes Tribute Cremation at $4,875 (urn included). Gentleman publishes Gathering and Memorial Service at $4,975. Braman’s traditional funeral is $5,235 with no casket; Gentleman’s is $6,585 with no casket. Kahler-Dolce’s Tribute Funeral package is $11,665 and includes a casket. Nebraska’s full-burial average ($8,620) usually includes a casket.

The plot is never in these packages

None of the figures in the table are cemetery property or opening/closing. At Westlawn-Hillcrest, ground burial property starts at $2,495. Ask the office: 402-556-2500.

Use the estimator for the extras

Flowers, death certificates, stationery, honorarium, catering, and miscellaneous almost never come in the package. The final expense estimator adds them separately, in those same fields.

The burial plot is a separate bill

The prices in the table above are from the funeral home. They do not include a burial plot (the grave space in the ground).

If there is a burial, the cemetery sends its own bill. That bill is usually:

  • the grave space (the plot)
  • opening and closing the grave
  • almost always a vault and a grave marker

Those items are not in the funeral packages.

Where to buy a plot in Omaha

You buy the plot from the cemetery. In Omaha you can call:

  • Westlawn-Hillcrest Memorial Park — 5701 Center St. Same office as the Dignity funeral home. Phone 402-556-2500.
  • Forest Lawn Memorial Park — 7909 Mormon Bridge Rd. Phone 402-451-1000. They do not post plot prices on the website; you have to call.
  • Catholic Cemeteries of the Archdiocese of Omaha — Calvary, Resurrection, and others. Counselor line 402-391-3711. Ask for a map and prices; they do not publish figures online.

Westlawn-Hillcrest does publish a starting price for ground burial. Forest Lawn and the Catholic cemeteries do not post figures. Call and ask for the current list in writing.

Westlawn-Hillcrest publishes ground burial property from $2,495 (Dignity cemetery property). Opening and closing, the vault, and the marker are still extra. People reselling spaces in that park usually ask less than the cemetery’s list.

Buying a resale plot (usually cheaper)

If someone already owns a plot and will not use it, they can sell it. Private asking prices are usually lower than the cemetery’s list. The cemetery still charges a transfer fee (ads cite $295–$495) and must put the deed in the buyer’s name.

Omaha FAQs

Is it the same as burial or funeral insurance?

Yes. Final expense, burial, and funeral insurance usually describe the same smaller permanent life policy. In Omaha the check is paid in cash to your beneficiary; it is not tied to one funeral home or one plot.

Do you serve Bellevue and other towns near Omaha?

Yes. Official quotes are by phone for Nebraska residents in Omaha, Bellevue, Papillion, La Vista, Ralston, and Elkhorn. There is no public walk-in office.

Do I need a medical exam?

Many final expense policies in Nebraska use health questions and no exam. If health is a barrier, guaranteed acceptance may be available, usually with a waiting period.

Does the policy pay from day one?

If you are approved for simplified issue, the full benefit typically pays from day one on covered claims. Guaranteed issue pays a limited benefit for the first two or three years, then the full benefit.

How much coverage is usually enough for cremation in Omaha?

With direct cremation published from $1,755 at Braman, $2,795 at Kahler-Dolce, and $2,870 at John A. Gentleman, many families choose $5,000 to $10,000 for the service, urn, travel, and small bills. A traditional burial often needs more: Braman’s GPL lists the traditional funeral package at $5,235, with no casket, and the plot, vault, and marker are extra.

What is the difference between a prepaid funeral and this insurance?

A prepaid plan is tied to one funeral home or plot and may lock that price. Final expense insurance pays cash to your beneficiary. They can use it at Braman, Kahler-Dolce, John A. Gentleman, Westlawn-Hillcrest, Forest Lawn, or for other final bills.

Does the funeral include the cemetery plot?

No. The funeral home price is one bill. The plot is another. At Westlawn-Hillcrest, ground burial property starts at $2,495. Ask the cemetery office for its list (402-556-2500). Resale plots can cost less; the cemetery still has to change the deed.
