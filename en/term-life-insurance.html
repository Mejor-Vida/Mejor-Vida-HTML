Term life insurance covers you for a set number of years

You pay a premium. If you die while the policy is in force and inside the term, the person you named receives the amount. When the term ends, that coverage ends. It is not a savings account. It is protection for a period you choose.

What to remember

  • Typical quotes cover 10, 15, 20, 25, or 30 years. A 40-year term or a 1-year term is not the usual offer from these appointed companies.
  • It is usually the lowest cost per dollar of death benefit. In our samples, $100,000 for 10 years at age 40 is about $8–$9 a month (non-tobacco, best illustrated class).
  • There is no guaranteed-acceptance term. Health questions apply. A simplified plan (no in-office exam) exists; fully underwritten term usually opens a better price and higher amounts.

Match the term to the years your family would still need your income or still carry a large debt. If the need does not end, also compare whole life or final expense.

What you are buying, in plain words

A term policy is not a prepaid funeral and not a savings account. It is a contract: the company pays a death benefit if you die during the term and premiums are current. The NAIC describes term as coverage for one or more years that generally does not build cash value.

What you buy

An amount (for example $100,000 or $500,000) that lasts for the term. Fully underwritten quotes typically start at $100,000.

Who is paid

The beneficiary you name. A bank or funeral home is paid only if that person chooses to pay them.

What you do not buy

It does not build cash value. If you outlive the term, premiums are not refunded unless you bought a return-of-premium rider, which raises the price and is not in these sample tables.

How it works, in four steps

  1. Match the years, then add up the dollarsPick a term that lasts as long as the need: years left on a mortgage, years until children finish school, or years a spouse would still rely on your income. A 30-year term costs more per month than a 10-year term at the same amount, because the company is covering a longer stretch. For the amount, add what would still need paying if you died this year: remaining mortgage, several years of pay, and education. Some advisers start with 5–8 times annual income, then add debts and subtract savings. The NAIC buyer’s guide prefers that personal add-up over a single formula. A worked example is further down.
  2. Answer health, tobacco, height, and weight honestlyThe company uses those answers — and often prescriptions and driving history — to set a price class. Tobacco in the last 12 months usually means a higher premium. Height and weight matter because they affect health class; they are not a separate product. A $250,000 or $500,000 term application typically asks for more detail than a $10,000 final-expense policy. Leaving out a condition can delay or affect a later claim. A simplified product (no in-office exam) still has health questions; there is no guaranteed-acceptance term.
  3. Pay a fixed premium for the years you boughtOn level term, the monthly premium stays the same during the original term if you pay on time. It does not rise just because you have a birthday. If a draft fails and the policy lapses, there is no death benefit. You are paying for protection those years, not depositing money you will get back.
  4. The family is paid only if you die during the termIf you die while the policy is in force, the person you named receives the face amount, usually free of income tax. If you outlive the term, that contract ends and premiums are not refunded (unless you bought a return-of-premium rider). Some appointed contracts let you convert to permanent coverage during a window in the policy, at the price for your age then — not the old term price.

How long a term you can apply for by age

The maximum age is not a web rumor. It is the highest cap among the appointed companies in these samples (non-tobacco). Tobacco lowers the cap. The same age may qualify for 10 years and not for 30.

Is this the right path?

It can fit if

  • There is a debt or an income with an end date: a mortgage, years of raising children, or replacing pay while dependents still rely on you.
  • You want a large death benefit for a lower monthly premium than whole life at the same face amount.

It often does not fit if

  • The goal is a funeral at 80 or a benefit that should not expire. Then compare final expense or whole life.
  • You cannot or will not answer health questions. Term is not issued “with no questions.” Guaranteed acceptance is a different, permanent product with a wait.

What term does not do

The term ends

If you outlive the contract, the company does not owe the face amount. You paid for protection during those years, not for an automatic refund.

Health matters more

Because the price per dollar is low, companies ask for more health detail than on a small final-expense policy. Simplified term exists; it is not guaranteed acceptance.

No cash value

You cannot borrow against the policy. If you cancel, there is usually no cash value to return.

Keeping coverage later costs more. Year-to-year renewal or a new policy at the new age is almost always much more expensive. Conversion to permanent, if the contract allows it, uses your age then — not the old term price.

How to think about the amount

The term face amount is the death benefit if you die during the term. It is not a savings goal. It is the money the person you named would receive to keep covering what your income pays for today.

  1. Who depends on your incomeStart with the people who would live on less if you died, and how many years they would still need that income.
  2. Add debts that would remainA mortgage is the usual one. Also count a car loan or another debt the family would still owe.
  3. Add a dated cost if it appliesChildren’s schooling, or another expense with a calendar. If it does not apply, skip it.
  4. Subtract what is already thereSavings, accounts, or other life insurance already in force. That money would already do part of the job.
  5. A shortcut is not a requirementSome advisers talk about several times annual pay. That is a shortcut, not a required number. The NAIC consumer guide starts from those same questions, not from one magic formula.

The result is a starting number for a conversation, not a promise and not an offer.

An example so the size is visible

If you contribute $55,000 a year and want to cover ten years of that income, that is $550,000. If $180,000 remains on a mortgage, add it. Subtract savings or other policies. The result — about $730,000 in this example — is a conversation, not a quote.

Can you switch to a permanent policy?

Some term contracts let you convert. That means you can move from that term policy to a permanent policy from the same company, using the health answers you already gave, without starting a brand-new medical exam for that conversion — if the contract still allows it and you convert in time.

People use this when the original term was bought for a period — a mortgage, years of income, children still at home — and later they still need coverage that should not expire. A spouse, final expenses, or another need with no end date is the usual reason.

  1. The price is not the old term priceYou pay the permanent product’s price at the age you convert. Someone who bought at 40 and converts at 55 pays the 55-year-old permanent rate, not the rate from the original term.
  2. There is a deadlineIt is in the contract. Miss it, and the conversion option is gone. The company does not add an extra day.
  3. Not every contract converts the same wayThe window, the products you can convert into, and the age limits sit in that policy. Appointed examples include Transamerica Trendsetter Super and Corebridge Select-a-Term; those are examples, not a full list.

Mejor Vida Insurance helps you read that window and choose the destination product before the deadline.

Two underwriting paths

Underwriting is the company checking health — and often prescriptions, height, weight, and tobacco — to decide whether to issue and at what price. Term has two real appointed paths. Neither is “no questions.”

Simplified / Easy Term

Health questions and a records check. No in-office exam. Faster: business days, not weeks of labs. Usually costs more per dollar. American Amicable Easy Term is the appointed example.

Start here if you want speed or dislike exams.

Fully underwritten

More questions. Depending on age and amount, labs or a paramed visit. Takes longer. Usually the lowest price per dollar on a large face. That is the path behind the illustrative tables on this page.

Start here if you want the lowest price per dollar on a large amount and can answer more.

Height, weight, and tobacco still matter on both paths.

On this site you can see illustrative prices and ask Mejor Vida Insurance to follow up. That is not the licensed application. A formal application comes later, with the company, and asks for more because it is a contract.

Height, weight, and tobacco are asked because they set a price class. They are not idle questions: they move the premium on both underwriting paths.

Frequently asked questions

If I outlive the term, do I get my money back?

No, unless you bought a return-of-premium rider at issue, which raises the price. The tables on this page are level term without that rider. You paid for protection during those years.

If I cancel, is there a refund?

On term with no cash value, canceling usually ends coverage with no money back. Premiums already used paid for the risk of those months.

Does term insurance have cash value?

Ordinary level term does not. Whole life can build cash value, with different premiums and rules.

Does term life require a medical exam?

It depends on the product and the amount. Easy Term does not require an in-office exam. Large fully underwritten term often asks for more data and sometimes labs.

Can you renew when the term ends?

Some contracts allow year-to-year continuation at a new price, almost always much higher. Do not count on renewing at the old price. Conversion to permanent, if it exists, has a date in the contract.

Can someone with health problems qualify?

Sometimes, with a higher premium or a smaller amount. There is no “no-questions” term. If the term questionnaire does not issue, final expense or guaranteed acceptance may be the other path — those are different products.

Does the premium rise every year?

During the original term of a level product, not just because of a birthday. Issuing later does cost more. Renewal or conversion uses your age then.

Why does the company keep the premiums if there was no claim?

Because the contract paid for the risk of those years, the same way auto insurance pays for a year with no crash. It is not a savings account.

How long does an application take?

An online quote is often immediate. A simplified application often resolves in business days. Fully underwritten coverage with labs takes longer. There is no fixed “four to six weeks”: it depends on the company and whether exams are required.
