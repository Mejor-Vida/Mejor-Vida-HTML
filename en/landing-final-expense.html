Did you know death also comes with expenses?

$8,000 – $12,000 average cost of a funeral in the United States

When a loved one passes away, funeral, burial, flowers, and other costs can add up to thousands of dollars — and that does not include outstanding debts or medical bills.

The federal government pays only $255 as a Social Security death benefit. That is not even enough for flowers.

If you were gone today, who would pay that bill? Your spouse? Your children? Do they have that money available?

That burden should not fall on your family.

What is final expense insurance?

Final expense insurance is a special type of permanent life insurance. It has a very specific purpose: to create funds to cover the costs that come when someone passes away.

Unlike other insurance, it does not expire. No matter how long you live — your coverage will always be there.

The money can be used for:

  • Funeral and burial expenses
  • Flowers and cemetery services
  • Outstanding medical bills
  • Credit card debt
  • Any other expense your family needs to cover

Your family decides how to use the money. It is not tied to a specific funeral home.

Can you qualify even if you have health issues?

The answer, in most cases, is yes.

There are two types of final expense policies:

For most people

Simplified-issue life insurance

  • Only requires answering a few health questions
  • No medical exam
  • Coverage begins from day one
  • Available even if you have diabetes, high blood pressure, or other conditions
For more serious health conditions

Guaranteed-issue life insurance

  • You cannot be declined for any reason
  • No health questions, no medical exam
  • There is a 2-year waiting period for full coverage
  • If something happened during that time, your family receives everything you paid plus about 10%

The vast majority of people qualify for simplified-issue insurance. Julie will review your situation and find the best option for you.

How much does final expense insurance cost?

The cost depends on your age, health, and the coverage level you choose. But there is good news:

$33 – $65 per month for a $10,000 policy for a 65-year-old
Premiums never increase once your policy is in place, your monthly payment is fixed forever
Up to age 85 you can qualify regardless of your health status

The younger and healthier you are when you buy your policy, the lower your monthly premium will be forever. Waiting always costs more.

A $10,000 policy can cost as little as $1 a day.

How does it work when your family needs it?

When the insured person passes away, your beneficiary — the person you designate — receives the money directly. They do not have to go through a funeral home. They do not have to wait months. The money arrives tax-free and can be used for whatever is needed.

The companies Julie works with — Mutual of Omaha, Assurity, and American Amicable — have been paying claims reliably for more than 100 years. They are among the most financially sound institutions in the world.

Julie's biography

Born and raised in Bogotá, Colombia, Julie has always had a deep passion for serving and supporting the Hispanic community. After meeting her husband in Colombia, they moved to Nebraska to build a new life together.

She arrived in the United States without speaking English, without knowing how to drive, and without extended family nearby—only her husband, a new culture to discover, and new beginnings to face. Like many immigrant families, she had to learn to adapt, grow, and move forward one step at a time. That experience gave her a profound understanding of the challenges, fears, and hopes many Hispanic families carry while building a life here.

That same experience deepened her desire to serve and stand alongside the Hispanic community.

A path of service

Her service journey began with Hispanic mothers through MilkWorks, where she educated, guided, and supported families on breastfeeding and early motherhood. Later, she worked with local libraries to promote literacy in the Hispanic community through bilingual reading programs and family story times—creating spaces where parents and children could learn and grow together.

As her impact grew, Julie took on an even larger mission: helping launch and lead a Spanish-language Christian radio station in Nebraska. Through radio, she brought messages of hope, faith, and a deeper connection with God into the daily lives of many Hispanic families.

Her mission today

Today, Julie continues that same spirit of service in a new and deeply meaningful way: helping Hispanic families understand why life insurance and final expense planning matter—and how to protect the people they love most.

Why this work is personal

For Julie, this work is deeply personal.

When Julie's father passed away, he did have a final expense policy. Still, the coverage was not enough to meet all the costs the family faced. While grieving someone they loved, they also had to carry the stress and financial weight for years.

But Julie also saw the other side of being prepared.

After her dad's death, Julie's mother faithfully kept her own final expense protection in place for many years. When the time came, that planning became a final act of love and care for the family. Instead of worrying about money in the middle of grief, the family could focus on mourning, supporting one another, and honoring her life—without the added pressure of debt and bills.

That experience left a lasting mark on Julie's heart.

Educating and guiding families

Now, her passion is to educate and guide Hispanic families across Nebraska and the United States who may not fully understand how life insurance works—or why it matters. Her goal is simple: help families find peace of mind, protect the people they love most, and leave a legacy of care, dignity, and love.

Julie believes every family deserves to be informed, prepared, and protected—with compassion, honesty, and guidance they can trust.

Today, Julie is ready and honored to serve families with the same care, understanding, and heart that shaped her own story.
