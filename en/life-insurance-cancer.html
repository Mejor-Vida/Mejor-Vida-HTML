Final expense insurance if you have or had cancer

The NCI explains that cancer is a group of diseases where cells grow out of control. For final expense, the useful question is not “did you ever have cancer?” but whether treatment is happening now, when it ended, and what kind. Basal skin cancer often stays on simplified issue. Active treatment for breast, lung, or colon cancer usually blocks a level plan.

What it is Underwriting What changes Cost Companies Questions

Three facts to start with

  • At appointed companies, cancer in active treatment is on the list of situations where simplified issue often cannot issue. Then we look at GIWL (ages 50–80, two-year wait for natural death).
  • On Transamerica’s chart: onset within 2 years, metastatic, recurrent, multiple cancers, or lymph nodes: decline. Cancer-free and no treatment in the last 2 years: Standard. Basal cell is not in that “other than basal” row.
  • Some skin cancers or very early stages may still go simplified. That is confirmed with the type and the prescriptions, not with an internet headline.

Give the type, whether you are on chemo, radiation, or pills, and the date of the last treatment. A “no” when oncology prescriptions say “yes” voids the claim.

What the insurer needs to know

The NCI separates a local tumor, one that has spread, and one that has come back. You do not have to translate stage into insurance jargon. Say the name the oncologist used, whether there is metastasis, and whether treatment is still going.

Prescription databases flag methotrexate, oral chemotherapy, and other oncology drugs. Accendo and other companies review them with the questions. Do not skip a medication “because it is for the skin.”

How appointed companies look at it

Living Promise, Accendo, and Americo ask about cancer and recent treatment; a “yes” to active treatment usually takes the case off level. Transamerica publishes time windows: two years from onset or treatment. Metastatic, recurrent, or multiple cancers: Decline on that chart. Cancer-free and off treatment 2 years: Standard, if it is the only factor. Hodgkin and lymphoma are read as cancer.

GIWL does not ask the cancer type. In return there is always a two-year wait for non-accidental death. If you are already off treatment for the time the simplified product asks, quote the immediate plan first: it is usually cheaper.

What usually changes the answer

The year and the type weigh more than the word “cancer.”

  • Treatment in progress (chemo, radiation, immunotherapy, or active hormone therapy depending on the form): simplified issue often cannot issue; GIWL if you are ages 50–80.
  • Last treatment more than two years ago, no recurrence: Transamerica can be Standard. Other companies have their own windows; we confirm them on the application.
  • Non-melanoma skin cancer (basal): often stays simplified. Melanoma is reviewed with the other cancers, not as “just skin.”
  • Several different cancers, or childhood cancer on a minor: Transamerica’s chart declines. An adult with one old cancer is not that case.

Frequently asked questions

I am on chemotherapy. Can I buy final expense?

Yes. At the companies we quote, the usual path is guaranteed acceptance (Corebridge GIWL, ages 50–80), not a level plan. There is a two-year wait for natural death. A covered accident can pay sooner, according to the contract.

I finished treatment three years ago. Is there a wait?

At Transamerica, cancer-free and off treatment 2 years can be Standard (immediate benefit if they issue). Other companies have their own windows. Quote with the real date.

Does skin cancer send me to GIWL?

Many basals do not. Melanoma and any cancer that reached lymph nodes are reviewed under cancer rules, not as a simple mole.

Should I wait for remission to apply?

If you want level, you sometimes have to wait out the product’s window. If you need something now, GIWL can issue within ages 50–80 without those questions. Waiting also raises age and premium.

Does this insurance pay for cancer treatment?

No. This is life insurance: a check to the beneficiaries when you die. It is not a health plan or a “cancer plus” supplement. Accendo Level can include an accelerated benefit for terminal illness on the level plan, with rules and a cap; it does not replace treatment.

I had two different cancers. What happens?

At Transamerica, multiple cancers are a decline on that chart. Then we look at GIWL if you qualify by age. Do not send simplified issue blindly.
