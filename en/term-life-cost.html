Term life insurance rates by age

How does term life insurance work?

Term covers you for a set period — for example 10, 20, or 30 years (or other lengths a carrier offers). If you die while the policy is in force and premiums are current, beneficiaries receive the death benefit per the contract.

When the term ends, coverage usually stops. Some policies allow renewal or conversion to permanent coverage (at new premiums). An optional return-of-premium rider may return some or all premiums at the end of the term if no claim was paid — it typically raises the cost and is not offered by every carrier.

Which factors determine the cost of term life insurance?

In general, price reflects the risk the insurer takes. Race, ethnicity, sexual orientation, marital status, credit score, and education level are not typically used to set premiums.

Gender

In most states, women usually pay less than men of the same age and profile.

Age

Quoting younger is usually cheaper. Age at issue drives the price.

Health history

Conditions, medications, and underwriting results can raise the premium or limit options.

Tobacco / nicotine

Cigarettes usually place you in a smoker class. Other forms depend on the carrier. These tables assume non-tobacco.

Amount & term length

More coverage or a longer term usually means a higher monthly premium.

Underwriting & riders

Exam vs. no-exam paths, plus optional extras (e.g. accidental death), change the cost.

Alcohol habits

Heavy use or related history can affect rating. Honest answers on the application protect a future claim.

Lifestyle, occupation, and hobbies

High-risk jobs or hobbies (private aviation, extreme climbing, etc.) can raise the price or require exclusions.

Type of underwriting

Fully underwritten (with exam) often unlocks better cost per dollar. Simplified or accelerated issue (no exam) is faster and often somewhat more expensive.

Policy riders (optional extras)

Accelerated benefits, accidental death, child coverage, or return of premium can add cost. Choose only riders that clearly fit your family’s needs.

Term life rates vs whole life rates

For the same face amount, term usually costs less each month because it covers a limited period. Whole life is permanent and, on many plans, builds cash value — so the premium is usually higher. Below, the “Term” column uses 10-year rates (educational comparison).

How to qualify — and how it affects cost

Simplified issue / accelerated underwriting (no exam)

Health questionnaire and, often, prescription or driving-record checks. No blood draw or paramed visit. Usually faster; cost per dollar can be somewhat higher than fully underwritten coverage.

Fully underwritten (with exam)

Collects more data (health history, sometimes urine/blood, height/weight). Takes longer, but often unlocks better cost per dollar and higher face amounts.

Important

There is generally no guaranteed-issue term life product (no health questions). Guaranteed acceptance usually applies to certain permanent / final-expense plans, almost always with a waiting period.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average cost of a term life policy?

It depends on age, gender, health, face amount, and term length. On this page’s tables (non-tobacco, fully underwritten Preferred Best), a younger adult may see single-digit or low double-digit monthly premiums for $100,000 of 10-year term; older ages or higher faces cost more. Quote for your real profile.

Does term life insurance premium increase with age?

Age at issue matters: buying later usually costs more. After issue, many term policies keep a level premium for the term length — it does not rise just because you get older while the policy stays in force. Renewal or conversion premiums usually reflect your then-current age.

Is term cheaper than universal life?

Often yes, per dollar of pure death benefit: term is not designed as a flexible accumulation vehicle. Universal / IUL depends on funding and charges and can cost more or behave differently. Compare with a real quote and illustration.

What happens when a term life policy ends?

Coverage usually stops. There is no automatic return of premiums unless you have a return-of-premium rider. You may be uninsured for that need, or explore renewal/conversion if the contract allows.

If you renew or convert, does the price go up?

Almost always the new premium reflects your current age and health, so it is usually higher than the original term premium. Converting to permanent coverage also changes the product type and cost.
