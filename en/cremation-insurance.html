Cremation insurance is a small life insurance policy

You are not buying a funeral package. You buy a life insurance policy. When you die, the person you name receives cash. They can use that cash for cremation — or for anything else.

What it is How it works Waiting Cremation cost Premium Vs. prepaid FAQ

What to remember

  • It is the same kind of policy as final expense or burial insurance. Companies do not sell a policy that pays only if there is a cremation.
  • The family gets cash, not a reserved cremation. They choose the funeral home. Leftover money stays with them.
  • Most people buy $5,000 to $15,000 of coverage. Direct cremation is often around $2,200. A cremation with a service is often around $6,300.

Think of the policy as money for the family. A prepaid plan is money already paid to one funeral home.

What you are actually buying

Families search for “cremation insurance” when they want to leave money for a cremation without asking their children to pay. At the companies Mejor Vida Insurance quotes, that product is final-expense whole life: a smaller permanent policy with a level premium if you keep it in force.

What you buy

A smaller whole life policy. It does not expire after 10 or 20 years the way term can.

Who is paid

The beneficiary you name. The crematory is not paid unless that person chooses to pay them.

What they can spend it on

Cremation, a service, medical bills, travel, or other needs. The contract does not pick the urn or the funeral home.

How it works, in four steps

  1. You applyThere is no lab visit on the simplified plans we quote. You answer health questions. The insurer reviews those answers and its databases.
  2. You pay a monthly premiumThe price depends on your age, sex, tobacco use, and health — not on the word “cremation.”
  3. The named person files a claimWhen you die, they contact the insurer (or Mejor Vida Insurance) with the death certificate and policy number.
  4. They receive cashThey pay for the cremation if they want. If money is left over, they keep it. If the bill is larger, they cover the gap.

Does the family have to wait two years?

The wait depends on how you qualify on the application, not on cremation as the goal. A level plan is the one you get when your health answers qualify: the full amount can pay from day one. Guaranteed acceptance skips health questions, but usually waits two years for natural death.

If you qualify after health questions

A covered death can pay the full amount from day one (contract exclusions still apply, such as suicide in the contestable period). See no-waiting-period coverage.

If you do not qualify and only guaranteed acceptance is offered

There is usually a two-year wait for natural death. In that window the family typically receives premiums paid plus contractual interest. See guaranteed acceptance.

What you pay each month for the policy

On appointed companies, a level $10,000 plan at age 50 is often about $28 a month for a woman and $34 a month for a man (non-tobacco, good health). $5,000 at the same age is often about $14 and $17. If the budget cannot cover a prepaid funeral, a small whole life policy is the path we quote. If you already paid a funeral-home plan and it covers what you want, you may not need a new policy of the same size.

Is this a fit?

It may help if

  • You want a cremation (or a simple service) and do not have that cash set aside.
  • You want the family to receive money they can spend anywhere, not a package locked to one funeral home.

You may not need a new policy if

  • You already prepaid, or you already have a permanent policy of about the same size.
  • Term that ends at 70 or 80 is usually a poor substitute: the term ends and the cremation does not.

Frequently asked questions

Is cremation insurance different from final expense?

No. It is the same class of smaller whole life. “Cremation” describes how the money is used, not a separate contract.

Does a life policy I already have cover cremation?

Yes: the payout is cash. The beneficiary can use it for cremation. Final expense is sized for a funeral or cremation, not to replace a paycheck.

Do I need a medical exam?

On the simplified plans we quote, no. There are health questions. See no medical exam.

Where do you buy it?

Through a licensed agent who compares companies, or sometimes direct to consumer by TV or mail. Mejor Vida Insurance quotes appointed companies and tells you whether there is a wait or day-one benefit. Licensed states are on the licenses page.

Is it worth it if I already have savings?

If the cash covers the cremation you want, you may not need a new policy. It helps when that fund is not there, or when you want the payout to skip probate if the beneficiary is named correctly.

How do you file a claim?

The beneficiary contacts the insurer or Mejor Vida Insurance with the death certificate and policy number. Payment goes to the named beneficiary, not automatically to the crematory. Keep the policy where the family can find it.

Until what age can I buy it?

It depends on the product. Many appointed companies issue through 85; Accendo Level can go through 89. See the age limit guide.

How much coverage should I buy?

For a direct cremation, $5,000 is often enough in many markets. With a service, $10,000 is a common starting point. Add debts or travel if you want a cushion.

Will beneficiaries owe tax?

In most cases the death benefit is not taxable income. This is not tax advice. A CPA should review large estates.

Is it better than a prepaid plan?

It depends. Prepaid locks services at a funeral home. The policy leaves unrestricted cash and a lower monthly premium, with underwriting. Compare both if you already have a closed budget with one provider.

Is term a good way to cover cremation?

It is usually a poor fit. Term ends; cremation does not. If the term runs out at 70 or 75, you can be left without coverage.

Can I buy this for a parent?

You can be the owner or the payer. The person whose life is insured almost always has to sign, answer health questions, and consent. Do not hide medical history.
