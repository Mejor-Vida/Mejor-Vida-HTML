The waiting period: what the policy pays at the start

It is the stretch at the start of some policies when a death from illness may pay less than the full amount. Not every policy has one. If you qualify for an immediate full-benefit plan, that wait does not apply.

Updated: September 6, 2026 ·

Key takeaways

  • Waiting period: time at the start when payment for non-accidental death is limited or is a return of premiums plus contract interest.
  • It often lasts about two or three years, depending on the product — there is no single national number.
  • An accident the contract covers usually pays the full amount during that time.
  • A full-benefit plan, if issued, does not use that wait for a covered natural death.

Hearing “waiting period” does not mean the policy does not exist. It exists. What waits is the full check for a death from illness. That distinction prevents a surprise at claim time.

What it is — and what it is not

A waiting period is the time at the start of some policies when a non-accidental death may pay less than the face amount. It is not a grace period, which is extra time to pay a late premium. It is also not the time the company takes to approve the application.

Which plans have one

It shows up on limited-early-benefit plans (modified or graded) and on guaranteed acceptance. It does not show up on a Living Promise Level, Accendo Level, Immediate Solution, or Eagle Select Level issued as a full benefit. Mixing those paths in one sentence is the most common mistake in ads.

Four questions before you sign

  • How many years does the wait last?
  • If death is from illness in year 1, do they return premiums, pay a percentage of the amount, or use another rule?
  • Does a covered accident pay 100% from day one?
  • Does the clock start on the issue date or on the first payment?

If your offer is not one of these names, do not copy the row. Ask for the contract.

Durations anchored to products
ProductTypical wait (non-accidental death)
Living Promise with a waitTwo years (premiums + 10%), then 100%
American Amicable, limited early yearsTwo years of percentages (30% / 70%), 100% in year 3
Accendo modifiedTwo years (typical return of 110% of earned premiums), 100% in year 3
Corebridge GIWL (quoted line)About two years (premiums + contract interest)

“About two to three years” in general language covers this product family. It is not a federal law.

Prior health and the wait

The wait does not erase an illness that already existed. The form already asked about it at issue. A false answer can harm a claim. If a diagnosis is very recent, the option that will issue is sometimes a waiting-period plan, or waiting out the time an immediate plan requires before you apply again. That is product-specific; there is no single national rule.

Common questions

Can I buy two policies to skip the wait?

Buying two waiting-period plans does not turn year 1 into 100%. An immediate plan at another company, if you qualify, does avoid that wait.

Is the wait the same as a grace period?

No. A grace period is extra time to pay a late premium. The waiting period is a rule about how the benefit is calculated at the start.

Does it start over if I change owners?

It depends on the contract. We assume neither a reset nor a continuation. Read that policy.
