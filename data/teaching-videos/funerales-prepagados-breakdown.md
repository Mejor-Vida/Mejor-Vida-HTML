## Teaching spine (page order)

What the article teaches. Video will keep the spine, not every paragraph.

1. A **funeral prepagado** (also called **previsión funeraria**) is an agreement with a funeral home: you choose goods and services now and pay part or all before they are needed. **It is not life insurance.**
2. Planning and prepaying are two decisions. You can write preferences without giving money to one funeral home.
3. Families want to spare children the bill and the choices. One path is sign now with a funeral home. Another is leave cash to someone they trust. They sound alike; they are not the same contract — especially if the family moves, the plan changes, or the business closes.
4. Someone still has to pay care of the body, a resting place, and a service if wanted. **Medicare does not pay that bill.** Social Security may pay **$255 once** if its rules are met; that does not cover a typical funeral.
5. You do not buy a generic “funeral.” You buy the **lines named in the contract** (casket or urn, transfer, viewing, staff) from the **lista de precios generales (GPL)** — the written menu the funeral home must give you.
6. FTC Funeral Rule still applies when planning ahead: buy only what you need; prices in writing. You may refuse a whole package; you may bring a casket or urn bought elsewhere. For cremation, a simple container is allowed; no state requires a display casket to cremate.
7. What is often **in** the funeral-home contract vs **apart** (plot, opening/closing, vault if the cemetery requires it, marker, flowers, obituary, travel). Do not assume “prepaid funeral” includes the cemetery. Funeral Rule usually does **not** cover a cemetery with no funeral home on site, or outside casket/monument sellers.
8. How the contract is built: get the GPL first; compare at least two homes; mark only the lines you want; written itemized statement **before** you pay. If paying in installments, read what happens if you stop, extra charges, and whether line prices are fixed. No national interest rate on this page.
9. Where the money goes is **state law**, not one federal guarantee. Some states require a share in a regulated **fideicomiso**; some contracts use **life insurance assigned to the funeral home**. Protections are not the same in every state; some state laws offer little effective protection (FTC). If they will not explain in writing which path this contract uses, do not sign. If the home closes or is sold, protection depends on those rules and the contract text.
10. Paying now does **not** automatically freeze today’s price. Ask in writing whether price is fixed at purchase or calculated at death. If it is not guaranteed, the family may owe a **complemento**. The page does **not** claim prepaid funerals “always” beat inflation.
11. Moving: FTC says some plans can be transferred, often with extra cost; others stay tied to that home. Cash from a life policy travels with the person named.
12. Canceling: depends on contract and state. **Irrevocable** cannot be undone at will. A cancelable one may return some or all, sometimes minus fees.
13. Tell the family the contract exists and where it is. FTC: if nobody knows you already paid, they can pay for the same funeral again. Do not leave the only copy in a will or a safe-deposit box nobody can open that weekend.
14. Size check (not a prepaid quote): NFDA **2023** medians **$8,300** viewing+burial and **$6,280** viewing+cremation, funeral-home side; plot/vault/marker usually extra. Local GPL is the real figure. Direct cremation without service is usually a smaller bill — the contract should say that, not a “traditional package.”
15. If what they want is **cash**, not a package: **seguro de gastos finales** is a small whole-life policy; the **beneficiario** receives a death benefit and decides how to spend it. It does not reserve a service at a named funeral home. If they already prepaid what they want, they may not need a new policy of the same size.

## Terms to define once

- funeral prepagado / previsión funeraria → contract with a funeral home: choose goods and services now, pay part or all before they are needed
- lista de precios generales (GPL) → written menu the funeral home must give you
- Funeral Rule (FTC) → you may buy only what you need; prices in writing; you may bring a casket or urn bought elsewhere
- fideicomiso → money held under state rules (ask what percentage, who the trustee is, who keeps the interest)
- seguro asignado a la funeraria → a life policy whose payout is assigned so that funeral home is named to collect and provide the service
- irrevocable → the arrangement cannot be undone at will
- seguro de gastos finales → small whole-life policy; cash to the person you named, not a reserved funeral-home package
- beneficiario / beneficio por fallecimiento → the person who receives the cash; the cash amount they receive
- prima → the regular policy payment

## Must-keep facts (page only)

- Prepaid funeral ≠ life insurance. Agency does not operate a funeral home; each home sells its own contract; states regulate them.
- Medicare does not pay the funeral bill. Social Security: **$255** once if rules are met — not a typical funeral.
- GPL before choosing; compare at least two homes; itemized statement before paying.
- Cemetery plot, opening/closing, vault, marker often separate. Funeral Rule usually does not cover a stand-alone cemetery.
- No state requires a display casket for cremation.
- State law governs prepaid funds; some states use a trust, some assigned insurance; protections vary; FTC: some state laws offer little effective protection.
- Price freeze is not automatic. Family may owe a complemento if not guaranteed in writing.
- Transferring after a move: sometimes possible, often extra cost (FTC).
- Tell the family; otherwise they may pay twice (FTC).
- NFDA 2023 medians $8,300 / $6,280 as **size of a viewing funeral**, not a prepaid-plan price. Page refuses a national “prepaid plans cost X–Y” range.
- If they already prepaid what they want, they may not need a new policy of the same size.
