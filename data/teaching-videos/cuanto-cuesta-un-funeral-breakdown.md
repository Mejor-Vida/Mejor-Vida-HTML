## Teaching spine (page order)

What the article teaches. Video will keep the spine, not every paragraph.

1. A funeral bill is usually more than one charge (funeraria, cementerio, extras the family chooses).
2. Four common paths: funeral with service + burial; burial with no viewing; funeral with service + cremation; cremation with no service. Price follows that choice, not one “U.S. average.”
3. Someone has to pay care of the body, a resting place, and a service if the family wants one. **Medicare does not pay that bill.** Social Security may pay **$255 once** if its rules are met — that does not cover a typical funeral.
4. Two different questions: what the funeral home charges vs what insurance costs to leave that money. This lesson is the first.
5. Inside the bill: FTC’s three arrangements people treat as one product — traditional / full-service funeral; direct burial; direct cremation. Labels are types of service, not a fixed price. Two homes in the same city can bundle different lines.
6. Three buckets: what the funeral home usually bills; what the cemetery usually bills; what the family adds (optional).
7. How it is quoted: ask for the **lista de precios generales (GPL)** before choosing. FTC Funeral Rule: buy only what you need; you may bring a casket or urn bought elsewhere (no refusal, no surcharge just for that). You do not have to buy a whole package. A spoken total should match the GPL. An internet average does not replace that sheet.
8. Two public number sets, different years, different things — do **not** mash into one “precio 2026.”
   - NFDA **2023 medians** (funeral with viewing): **$8,300** burial, **$6,280** cremation. Funeral-home side. Plot, vault, marker, flowers usually extra.
   - Funeralocity **July 2026** package averages (50 states + D.C.): full-service burial **$8,532**; burial no viewing **$5,109**; funeral with service + cremation **$6,246**; cremation no service **$2,217**. Skipping the viewing lowers the bill; direct cremation is the lowest of these four on average; city can sit outside that order.
9. Even after the funeral-home package, cemetery + extras can push the **total** well above $8,300 / $6,280.
10. Your city will not match the average (local competition, crematory, land, family choices). Ask for two or three GPLs. Calculator = state-level estimate, not an invoice.
11. These figures do not forecast 30 years out. Cremation becoming more common is a **choice mix**, not an automatic discount. No verified national median for “green” funerals on this page.
12. How families pay (pointer only): savings / account; funeral-home prepaid contract (not life insurance; hard to move if you relocate); cash from a policy (beneficiary decides; funeral home is paid only if that person pays them). Full lesson is the pay-for-a-funeral page.
13. If you want to leave cash, not a package: final-expense insurance is small whole life that pays cash to the named person — it does **not** reserve a service at a specific funeral home. Product/price belong on other pages.

## Terms to define once

- **Lista de precios generales (GPL)** → the written menu of goods and services the funeral home must give you before you choose.
- **Funeral Rule de la FTC** → federal rule that lets you buy only what you need, and bring a casket or urn bought elsewhere.
- **Mediana** → the middle value: half of cases below, half above (NFDA 2023).
- **Promedio** → add the values and divide (Funeralocity packages).
- **Funeral tradicional / de servicio completo** → transfer, care of the body, viewing, a service, then burial or cremation.
- **Entierro directo** → body to the cemetery with no viewing or funeral-home service.
- **Cremación directa** → cremation without that viewing; family may use a simple container instead of a display casket.
- **Cargo básico no declinable** → the basic services fee almost every funeral home publishes (name it once; do not invent the dollar amount).
- **Seguro de gastos finales** (only if the close needs it) → small whole-life policy that pays cash to the beneficiary; not a reserved funeral package. Do not teach premiums here.

## Must-keep facts (page only)

- Bill is usually more than one charge.
- Four common paths; price follows the path.
- Medicare does not pay the funeral. Social Security may pay **$255 once** if rules are met; that does not cover a typical sepelio.
- This video does **not** quote a funeral home.
- NFDA 2023: **$8,300** funeral with viewing and burial; **$6,280** with viewing and cremation. Plot, vault, marker, flowers usually extra.
- Funeralocity July 2026 national means of state packages: **$8,532** / **$5,109** / **$6,246** / **$2,217** for the four paths above. Direct cremation lowest of the four on average; full-service burial highest. A given city can sit outside that order.
- GPL before choosing. Buy only what you need. Spoken total should match the GPL.
- You may buy the casket or urn elsewhere.
- Cemetery plot is usually **not** inside the $8,300 median.
- Cremation with a viewing is still a large bill, only lower than burial with viewing.
- Do not publish a 30-year doubling forecast; the future is not on today’s GPL.
- Prepaid funeral ≠ life insurance. Policy cash goes to the beneficiary.
