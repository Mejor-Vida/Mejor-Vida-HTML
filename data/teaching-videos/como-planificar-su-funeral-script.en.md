## Spoken script

### The situation

Hi. I’m Jhenny, the educational assistant for Mejor Vida Insurance.

If you searched how to plan a funeral, you are probably trying to spare the family from guessing under pressure. After a death, there are usually only a few days to choose a funeral home and a type of service. A written plan does not take away the grief. It does reduce the choices the family has to invent while they are tired.

### Three things that sound alike

It feels like one job: I’ll take care of my funeral. People usually mean one of three things, and those things sound alike. One is to write what they want. One is to tell a daughter or a spouse. One is to pay a funeral home now. All three feel like the funeral is already planned. They are not the same thing.

### What a plan is

A plan, here, is written wishes: burial or cremation, whether there is a visitation, whom to call, and where the papers are. It is not, by itself, a contract. It is not a payment. The Federal Trade Commission explains that you may decide on arrangements without sending money to a funeral home in advance. You can leave those instructions now, and decide later whether the money comes from savings, a prepaid funeral-home contract, or a life insurance check. Those are different jobs.

### When it works

For that writing to work, three things have to be true. Someone has to have a plan. Someone has to know the plan exists. And those people have to be able to get to it right after the person dies — not later, when the funeral has already been arranged.

That is why a will is not enough on its own. A will is important for property. It is often read after the funeral. That is why a bank box is not enough as the only copy. The bank can be closed when the family needs the paper. And that is why “I told someone” is not enough on its own. That person may forget details, or may not be there. A short, dated writing, with copies in the hands of the people who will need it, is easier to follow.

Give copies to family, and to an attorney if you have one. Keep one copy where those people can reach it right after the death. Review it every few years, because wishes change.

### What the writing does not do

The writing guides whoever signs with the funeral home. It does not freeze future prices. It does not bind a funeral home that never signed it. And it does not replace cash. Paying a funeral home now, or leaving a life-insurance check, are different jobs.
