## Teaching spine (page order)

What the article teaches. Video will keep the job, not every paragraph.

1. Write what you want so the family is not guessing under pressure: burial or cremation, visitation or not, whom to call. You do **not** have to pay a funeral home now to leave those instructions.
2. You may decide arrangements **without paying in advance**. Prices can rise; a business can close; in some places prices can also fall. Review the writing every few years and make sure the family knows where it is.
3. Do not leave preferences **only in a will** (often read after the funeral) or as the **only** copy in a safe-deposit box (the bank may be closed when the family needs the paper). Give copies to family and to an attorney if you have one; keep one copy where those people can reach it right after the death.
4. NFDA **2023** medians ($8,300 visitation+burial; $6,280 visitation+cremation, funeral-home side) are the **size of a typical bill**, not a price for “planning.” Plot, vault, and marker are usually extra.
5. Planning and prepaying are two jobs. Instructions can be written now; money can come later from savings, a prepaid contract, a life policy, or a mix.
6. When someone dies there are usually only a few days to choose a funeral home, a type of service, and, if there is a burial, a cemetery space. A calm written note does not remove grief; it reduces choices invented on the spot.
7. Planning is not “settle everything with one funeral home today.” It is a record of what matters and what you do not want, so the person who signs knows where to start.
8. A **funeral plan** on this page = a record of preferences (body, visitation or service, whom to call, where papers are). By itself it is **not** a contract and **not** a payment.
9. A **prepaid funeral** = a contract with a funeral home (or, in some states, another licensed seller) for named goods or services, paid in part or in full before need. Money, moving, and canceling have their own rules — already filmed on the prepaid page.
10. FTC: you may decide arrangements without sending cash in advance. If you later prepay, ask where the money sits and what happens if you move or the firm closes — and tell the family so they do not pay twice.
11. Three parts of the record: there is a plan; someone knows it exists; they can get to it right after the death. How it will be paid can be decided later and does not have to live on the same paper.
12. Write in language the family understands. Name a funeral home or cemetery only if already chosen; if not, say “compare at least two funeral homes” and note the type of service. FTC: copies to family and attorney; one copy they can reach when they need it. Review every few years. If you prepaid, review **that contract** separately — it is not this preference note.
13. Three places that are not enough **on their own:** only the will; only the bank box; only “I told someone.” A short dated paper is easier to follow.
14. FTC names three common service paths (none required): traditional/full service (visitation + service, usually the most expensive on the funeral-home side); direct burial (no visitation, usually no embalming; funeral home/cemetery still charge transfer, container, open/close); direct cremation (no prior visitation; Funeral Rule: alternative container must be offered; a memorial can happen later). Culture, faith, and budget matter more than a national average.
15. Body donation and “green” burial may be written as preferences. Programs and cemeteries set their own rules; the page does not invent which accept, or a national green-burial price. Leave a contact number.
16. Six questions that fit on one paper (can also write “my daughter decides”): what happens to the body; visitation or rite; which funeral homes to compare / ask for the GPL; cemetery if burial; who pays and with what (name who has the papers — payment detail is the other video); who has copies and when to reread (e.g. every two or three years).
17. GPL: written menu a funeral home must show if you ask in person; by phone they must give prices you ask; they are **not** required to mail the GPL. Compare more than one. Packages allowed, but itemized list too. Funeral Rule covers funeral homes; generally **not** cemeteries/mausoleums unless that business also sells funeral goods/services.
18. In **most states**, FTC: you are **not legally required** to use a funeral home to plan or hold a funeral. Death certificate and permits are still **state** paperwork. The page does not list states. Many families still hire a funeral home for transfer and timing.
19. FTC: it often helps the family to buy a cemetery plot **before** need (location, faith of the grounds, monument/vault/flower rules). Open/close and perpetual care are often **apart** from the funeral-home bill. National cemetery burial for a Veteran is **not automatic**; confirm on VA.gov. A VA burial **allowance** is a different payment from a grave in a national cemetery. Skip dollar amounts in this video.
20. Planning has no national “price to plan.” $255 Social Security and Medicare-not-paying-the-funeral are payment facts already filmed — point at the mix-up only if needed, do not reteach the pay lesson.
21. What a writing usually helps: family knows the type of service and whom to call; they can compare a GPL without buying a whole package; a plot or VA cemetery to check can be noted before the rush.
22. What it does **not** guarantee: it does not freeze future prices unless you sign a prepaid contract with clear rules; it does not bind a funeral home that never signed the paper; it does not replace cash. If there are no savings, no prepaid contract, and no policy, the family still has to gather payment.
23. Planning vs prepaid vs life insurance: planning = preference writing; prepaid = named lines at a funeral home; life insurance = cash to the **beneficiary**, who decides where to spend it unless the payout is assigned to a provider.
24. If you already prepaid exactly what you want, you may not need a new policy of the same size. If you want the family to receive cash and choose the funeral home later, that is a quote — not a funeral-home quote.

## Terms to define once

- plan funerario (on this page) → written preferences: body, visitation or service, whom to call, where papers are; not a contract and not a payment
- funeral prepagado → contract with a funeral home for named goods or services, paid before need (other video)
- lista de precios generales (GPL) → written price menu the funeral home must show if you ask in person
- beneficiario → the person named to receive a life-insurance check

## Must-keep facts (page only)

- You can write funeral wishes without paying a funeral home now (FTC).
- Planning ≠ prepaid contract ≠ life-insurance cash.
- Will is often read after the funeral; a bank box may stay locked when the family needs the paper; telling one person is not enough.
- FTC: copies to family and attorney; keep one copy they can reach right after the death; review every few years.
- A preference paper does not freeze prices, does not bind an unsigned funeral home, and does not replace cash.
- Traditional / direct burial / direct cremation are examples of what to write, not a required package (direct cremation: alternative container must be offered).
- In most states you are not legally required to use a funeral home to plan or hold a funeral; permits still follow state rules.
- National-cemetery burial for a Veteran is not automatic (VA.gov). Skip if runtime is tight.
