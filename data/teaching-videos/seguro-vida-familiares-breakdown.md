## Teaching spine (page order)

What the article teaches. The subject is **otros familiares** — a relative who is not your parent. Aunt, uncle, cousin, in-law, niece, and nephew are examples of that class, not competing titles.

1. A **tío, un primo o un cuñado** (the page’s opening trio) can be someone whose **funeral you would pay**. That can meet the NAIC’s **interés asegurable**. What changes is **who may own** the contract.
2. Concrete example on this page: **Mutual of Omaha** simplified life. **Tíos, sobrinos y primos** appear as accepted **beneficiarios contingentes**, not on the short **owner** list. Each company publishes its own list. Mejor Vida Seguros confirms it before you apply.
3. Three starting facts: the **insured adult always signs** — there is no adult policy “without them knowing.” There is **no group burial plan** that covers several adults on one contract (each life, one policy). A **child or grandchild rider** on your own policy covers **minors**, not another adult relative (the page’s example: not your uncle/aunt).
4. If the relative is a parent, grandparent, or sibling, those owner rules are clearer (other guides). This lesson is the mismatch for **more distant relatives**.
5. Relationships companies usually recognize: NAIC talks about **immediate family**, and sometimes a **partner or a creditor**. On final expense, a funeral you would pay is the usual argument. A **neighbor or a friend with no financial loss** does not qualify.
6. Three levels (Mutual of Omaha simplified life; other companies differ), most control to least:
   - **Dueño o beneficiario:** can run the contract (change beneficiaries, ask the company questions) and can also receive the check. Typically: spouse, fiancé, domestic partner, parent, adult child, trust, business partner with paperwork.
   - **Solo beneficiario:** can receive the payment; **cannot own** and cannot discuss the contract with the company. Typically: siblings, grandparents/grandchildren, relatives by marriage, ex-spouse, the estate.
   - **Beneficiario contingente:** paid only if the primary beneficiary has already died. **Not an owner.** Typically: aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews, cousins and other more distant relatives; friends.
7. Other appointed companies (Transamerica, Aetna Accendo, Americo, Corebridge) keep their own lists. A **funeral amount** is usually simpler to place than a **large term** policy. Confirm the product — not a generic internet “yes.”
8. Why there is no “one policy for the whole family”: each adult is a different health risk; premiums, questions, and waits do not blend. Child riders and separate children’s policies exist; that does **not** cover a 62-year-old sister.
9. Sample cost: same final-expense bands. **Age is the insured relative’s.** Tabs on the page: $10,000 / $15,000 / $25,000. Do not recite the table.
10. How to start if the relationship is more distant: say the **exact relationship** and the **amount**. If the product will not let you own it, two honest paths: the **relative owns it and you pay** (you may not be able to ask the company questions), or **it is not issued and you save**. Do **not** sign an online form in someone else’s name: that can be a **crime** and it **voids the contract**.
11. FAQs that teach the same class, not only one relative: Can I insure an uncle? Sometimes, if there is insurable interest **and they sign**; you may only be allowed as beneficiary, not owner — same logic for an aunt, cousin, or in-law. Close friend? Usually out unless a documented loan or formal business. Workplace group life? Covers the employee, sometimes spouse/children per that plan — it does **not** replace a final-expense policy on another adult relative.

## Terms to define once

- interés asegurable (NAIC) → a recognized financial loss if that person dies (a funeral you would pay, a shared debt, or income they provided). Wanting to help is not enough. A stranger cannot buy.
- asegurado → the person whose death triggers the payment; they must sign
- dueño → the person who controls the contract (change beneficiaries, ask the company questions)
- pagador → the person whose account pays the premium (can be you even if you are not the owner)
- beneficiario → the person who receives the check
- beneficiario contingente → paid only if the primary beneficiary has already died; not an owner
- gastos finales → smaller whole-life amount meant for a funeral / small debts (page sample bands $10,000–$25,000)
- rider de hijo o nieto → add-on on an adult’s policy that covers **minors**, not another adult relative
- vida simplificada (Mutual of Omaha example) → the product whose owner/beneficiary lists this page quotes; other companies publish different lists

The page’s family hub names four roles (insured / owner / payer / beneficiary). This page’s body uses owner vs beneficiary vs contingent. Define those three in speech; **pagador** only if we keep the “you pay, they own” path (we should).
