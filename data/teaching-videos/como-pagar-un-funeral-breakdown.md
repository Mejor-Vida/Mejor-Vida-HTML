## Teaching spine (page order)

What the article teaches. Video will keep the job, not every paragraph.

1. When someone dies, the funeral home and cemetery expect payment for the goods and services the family chose. There is **no single government “funeral check”** that covers that bill.
2. Deciding the type of service and having the money ready are two jobs. You can write preferences without handing cash to one funeral home.
3. The bill does not wait for an estate to settle. Someone has to pay care of the body, a resting place, and a service if wanted.
4. **Funeral Rule (FTC):** written itemized statement of what you chose **before** you pay. There is **no national deadline** of “pay everything the day before the service.” Each funeral home sets its own due date. Ask for that date in writing with the GPL.
5. There is no federal program that pays “the funeral for free.” There are small or conditional benefits ($255 from Social Security; a VA burial allowance if the person was an eligible veteran) and ads that promise the opposite.
6. Paying a funeral means gathering money for the **lines the family chose** from the **lista de precios generales (GPL)** — the written menu the funeral home must give you. A funeral with visitation is not the same bill as cremation without a service.
7. Money can already be in a family account; already paid to a funeral home; arrive later as a policy check to the person named; be a small public benefit; or be borrowed. Combining sources is normal. **Confusing them** — treating a funeral-home contract as if it were insurance, or the reverse — is what leaves the family with two bills or a package they cannot move.
8. In practice, the funeral home bills **whoever signed** the services contract. Whether the estate can reimburse that person is **state law**. The page does **not** publish a national rule that “the children have to pay.”
9. Three places the money lives:
   - **Already the family’s** — savings, a joint account someone can use, or an informal collection. It is available when that person can use it — not when an insurer or an office finishes paperwork.
   - **Already sent to a provider** — a prepaid funeral buys the **named lines** in a contract with a funeral home. It is **not** a free check for the family.
   - **Arrives to a person** — a life policy pays the **beneficiario** (the person you name). That person decides whether to pay the funeral home, travel, or another debt.
10. How payment is assembled: get the GPL; mark only what you want; written total before you pay. FTC: you may bring a casket or urn bought elsewhere. Ask how they take payment (account, check, card, or the beneficiary paying there). Some funeral homes accept **assignment** of a policy payout to them — that home is named to collect and provide the service. That is an arrangement with **that** funeral home, not a rule of every policy.
11. If nobody knows there is already a prepaid contract or a policy, the family can pay for the same funeral again. Leave papers where they can be found that weekend — not only in a safe-deposit box.
12. Size check (not a city quote): NFDA **2023** medians **$8,300** viewing+burial and **$6,280** viewing+cremation, funeral-home side; cemetery and extras usually extra. A visitation funeral is thousands of dollars, not $255. Direct cremation without a service is usually lower — read the **local GPL**, not an internet average.
13. Six sources families **combine** (not a menu they must use all of):
    1. Savings or an account someone can use now (confirm it is not frozen when the owner dies). If that money is already set aside, a new product of the same size may be unnecessary.
    2. Prepaid funeral — named goods/services at one home, not “whatever burial costs.” Full contract lesson is a different video.
    3. Cash from life insurance — to the beneficiary; does not reserve a named funeral home unless the payout is assigned.
    4. Social Security **$255** once if a spouse or children meet the rules; must apply; not meant to cover a visitation funeral.
    5. VA help **if** the rules are met — not automatic for every veteran. Confirm on the VA site. (Dollar amounts on the page are date- and service-connection-specific; skip in this runtime unless Julie asks.)
    6. Card or loan — some homes accept a card; interest is the card issuer’s or lender’s, not a national funeral rate we can cite. Closes a gap but adds debt.
14. A friends/community collection can help. It is not a federal program with a published amount, so the page does not treat it as a figure to plan on.
15. Public help vs ads: Social Security $255 is not a funeral plan. VA is not automatic. **Medicare** pays medical care while the person is alive (hospice too, when its rules are met) — **not** the funeral, cemetery, or debts left at death. Add $255 and, if it applies, a VA allowance, and most of a visitation funeral can still be unpaid. Medicare does not close that gap.
16. Medicaid is run by each state. Some states have limited burial help for people with no resources; amounts are not national. Do not invent “Medicaid pays the funeral.” Ask the state Medicaid office if it might apply.
17. Borrowing: extra cost is interest and fees on **that** credit, not a single U.S. funeral surcharge. Ask if the home takes a card, if there is a card surcharge, and if **cash advances** (flowers, obituary, or other third-party charges the home pays out) can go on the card. FTC requires those advances to be itemized; it does not say every home accepts the same payment method. No national “funeral loan costs X–Y” range on this page.
18. A path **can fit** if: savings are enough and usable now; they want named service lines and are not moving (prepaid with the price in writing); they want a trusted person to receive cash and choose later (a life policy).
19. A path **falls short** if: they count only on $255 or “Medicare covers it”; the prepaid plan does not freeze price or cannot move and the family lives in another city; they close the gap with a card they cannot pay soon (interest can eat the cushion they meant to leave).
20. Five payment questions at the funeral home (in writing, with the GPL). They do not replace an insurance contract or a prepaid contract: total of chosen lines vs what is extra (cemetery, marker, flowers); when payment is due; transfer/check/card and any card fee; if there is a life policy, will they wait for the insurer or require assignment; if a prepaid contract already exists, where it is and what it still covers.
21. If what they want is **cash**, not a package: **seguro de gastos finales** is a small whole-life policy; premiums keep it in force; the beneficiary receives the death benefit and decides how to spend it. It does not reserve a service at a named home. If a policy is approved to pay from the start, the family can use that cash when needed. If the company only offers a wait for natural death, that wait is in the policy — the page does not guess it. If they already prepaid the package they want, they may not need a new policy of the same size.

## Terms to define once

- lista de precios generales (GPL) → written menu the funeral home must give you
- Funeral Rule (FTC) → written statement of what you chose before you pay; you may buy only what you need; you may bring a casket or urn bought elsewhere
- funeral prepagado → contract with a funeral home for named goods or services, paid in part or in full beforehand; not a free check for the family
- beneficiario / beneficio por fallecimiento → the person named to receive the policy cash; the cash they receive
- asignación (del pago del seguro a la funeraria) → that funeral home is named to collect the policy benefit and provide the service; not a rule of every policy
- seguro de gastos finales → small whole-life policy; cash to the person you named, not a reserved funeral-home package
- prima → the regular policy payment
- adelantos en efectivo → third-party charges the funeral home pays out (flowers, obituary, etc.); FTC says they must be itemized

## Must-keep facts (page only)

- No single government funeral check. No federal “free funeral” program.
- Funeral home bills whoever signed the services contract. No national rule that children must pay. Estate reimbursement is state law.
- Medicare is medical insurance for a living person (hospice too, when its rules are met). It does not pay the funeral, cemetery, or debts at death.
- Social Security: **$255** once if a spouse or children meet the rules; must apply; generally within two years; not a visitation funeral.
- NFDA 2023 medians $8,300 / $6,280 as **size of a viewing funeral** (funeral-home side), not the viewer’s invoice. Plot/vault/marker usually extra. Local GPL is the real figure.
- Prepaid funeral ≠ life insurance. Prepaid = named lines at a home. Life insurance = cash to the beneficiary.
- Combining sources is normal. Confusing a funeral-home contract with insurance is what causes a second bill or a package that will not move.
- FTC: itemized statement before you pay. Each home sets when money is due — not a national “day before” rule.
- Tell the family where the prepaid contract or policy is, or they may pay twice.
- Card/loan adds interest from that issuer/lender. No national funeral-loan rate on this page. Do not claim “most funeral homes require full payment before the service.”
- If money is already set aside and usable, a new product of the same size may be unnecessary.
- Agency does not operate a funeral home; this page does not quote a funeral home or a loan.
