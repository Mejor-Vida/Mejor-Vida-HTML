## Spoken script

### What it is

Hi. I’m Jhenny, the educational assistant for Mejor Vida Insurance.

A prepaid funeral is an agreement with a funeral home. You choose the goods and services now, and you pay some or all of that bill before they are needed. It is not life insurance. Planning and paying are also two different decisions. You can write your wishes down for the family without sending money to one funeral home.

### Why it matters

When someone dies, someone has to pay for care of the body, a resting place, and a service if the family wants one. That bill arrives quickly. Medicare does not pay it. Social Security may pay 255 dollars once, if its rules are met. That does not cover a typical funeral.

A lot of people want to spare their children that invoice and those choices. Some people sign with a funeral home now and pay in advance. Other people leave cash to someone they trust, so that person can pay later. Those two ideas sound alike, because both try to keep the family from scrambling. They are not the same thing. A prepaid contract is tied to one funeral home and to the items you named. Cash is not. That difference shows up if the family moves, if the plan changes, or if that business closes.

### How it works

When you prepay, you are not buying “a funeral” as one bundled product. You are buying specific items from that funeral home’s written menu — things like the transfer of the body, a visitation, staff, and a casket or an urn. That menu is called the general price list. The funeral home has to give it to you. Ask for it first. Compare at least two homes. Mark only the lines you actually want. Then get a written statement of each item and the total, before you pay.

A federal rule from the Federal Trade Commission still applies when you plan ahead. You may buy only what you need. You may bring a casket or an urn you bought somewhere else. And do not assume the cemetery is included. The plot, opening the grave, and the marker are often separate bills.

### What they need to know

The federal rule we just talked about covers how you shop. What happens to your money after you pay the funeral home is based on state law, not federal law. There is no one federal protection that works the same in every state.

Some prepaid contracts with a funeral home put a share of the payment into a regulated trust. Other prepaid contracts with a funeral home use a life insurance policy assigned to that same home, so the funeral home is named to collect the benefit and provide the service. The protections are not the same in every state. The FTC warns that some state laws offer little effective protection. If the funeral home will not put in writing which of those paths this contract uses, do not sign.

Paying the funeral home now also does not automatically freeze today’s price. Some prepaid funeral-home contracts lock the price in writing. Others set the price when the death occurs. If that is not guaranteed in the contract, the family may still owe a balance. Ask what happens if you cancel, if you move, or if that funeral home closes. Some of these contracts are irrevocable: they cannot be undone at will. Cash from a life policy travels with the person you named. A prepaid package at a funeral home usually does not.

### Mistakes to avoid

Tell the family the contract exists and where it is. If nobody knows you already paid, they can pay for the same funeral twice. Do not leave the only copy in a will or a safe-deposit box that no one can open that weekend.
