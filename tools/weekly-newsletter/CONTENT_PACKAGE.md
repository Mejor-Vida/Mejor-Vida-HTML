# Weekly content package (quality spec)

Last week’s reputable news → **three equal** consumer stories → website features, email, and Facebook.

Research finds **exactly 3** stories. Give them the **same depth**. Do not make one “the main article” and shrink the other two.

**Languages:** website and Julie’s email in **Spanish**; admin email in **English**; Facebook in **Spanish**. Same facts in every version.

**Audience:** Working individuals and families—especially Hispanic and bilingual families—with limited insurance knowledge. Explain clearly without talking down. The email is a **client nurture letter**, not a staff briefing or a news dump.

**How to explain:** Situation first, everyday words second, official name last. Never open with GINA, FTC, HHS, III, or another acronym. Tell the reader why a family looking at life insurance should care.

**Do not put in the letter** (Julie cut these; they sound weird to clients):
- Narrating the writing (“you do not need insurance language,” “we use everyday words first”)
- “If you already have a policy, a news story does not cancel it”
- “If you are applying, read every health question slowly”
- Narrating research (“we do not have that person’s file,” “we will not repeat a rate we did not verify”)
- Staff-memo lines (“three notes at the same depth”)

**Goal:** Educate first. For **each** story: what is happening, why, who could be affected, what it could mean for a family, what they can do. May mention Mejor Vida Insurance / Mejor Vida Seguros; must not feel like an ad.

Facebook follows `facebook-post-rules.md` (no blog URL in the main caption).

## Part 1 — Website news features (all three)

For **each** of the three stories, write an original **700–1,000 word** news-style educational feature.

Each feature includes:

1. Three accurate headline options  
2. A short subtitle (why it matters)  
3. A strong opening from a timely development, verified fact, relatable situation, or common problem  
4. What is happening  
5. Necessary background  
6. Unfamiliar terms explained  
7. Section: **Qué puede significar esto para su familia** / **What this could mean for your family**  
8. Section: **Qué puede hacer ahora** / **What you can do now** (3–5 practical steps)  
9. Balanced conclusion (one takeaway)  
10. Gentle CTA to learn more or review options with Mejor Vida Insurance / Seguros  

Style: trustworthy consumer news; short phone-friendly paragraphs; no fear, clickbait, or invented facts, quotes, statistics, laws, prices, or studies. Link claims to original sources. Separate verified facts from labeled hypotheticals. Coverage, eligibility, and price vary by person, carrier, state, and policy. No individualized legal, tax, medical, or financial advice. Only products Mejor Vida is authorized to discuss (final expense, term, whole life, IUL, annuities). Do not list licensed states in the body.

## Part 2 — Email newsletter

Adapt **all three** website features. Do not paste the full articles.

- Subject line (the week, not only one story)  
- Preview text  
- Warm introduction (~60–100 words; client nurture: thank them, say this letter is for families looking at life insurance. Do not narrate your writing method.)  
- **Each story: 175–250 words** (same depth for all three). Cover: what happened that week, who reported it, verified facts, why families care, what to understand, one practical takeaway, any limitation. Explain terms the first time.  
- One short closing lesson for the week with **one** soft Mejor Vida CTA (402-440-5438) — not after every story  
- Per-story button **Leer el artículo completo** / **Read the full story** — only if that URL is **already live**  
- Formal educational disclaimer at the end (template footer)

Julie = Spanish. Admin = English.

## Part 3 — Facebook (Spanish)

Follow `facebook-post-rules.md`.

Treat the three stories as a set, or post three equal captions. Do not spotlight only one.

**Version A — conversation post (about 100–175 words):** hook, why the week’s news could matter, one takeaway (or one line per story), dual CTA **INFO** / **REVISAR**, optional DM. **No URL in the caption.** First follow-up comment may include the site or article links.

**Version B — standalone educational post (about 200–300 words):** useful even without a click; **three** practical takeaways (one from each story); Mejor Vida only if natural; non-pressuring close.

Hashtags: 3–5. No emojis unless Julie asks.

## Part 4 — Publishing materials

For **each** story (same fields, three times):

- URL slug  
- SEO title ~50–60 characters (Spanish for the indexed page)  
- Meta description ~140–160 characters  
- Primary keyword + 3 related  
- Featured-image concept + alt text  
- Category  
- Source list with direct links  
- Date researched  
- Subject-appropriate disclaimer  

Before finishing: website, email, and Facebook must **not contradict** each other, and the three stories must stay the same length class.
