You can ask for a funeral price list. You do not have to buy a package.

September 6, 2026 | Mejor Vida Insurance | About 10 min read

The FTC says funeral homes must give you a written list of each charge. Use it to compare homes and to estimate how much final expense coverage your family might need.

Published: September 6, 2026 · Source: see links at the end of the article

When a family calls a funeral home, they often ask for “the price of the funeral” as if it were a single number. In practice, that number is often a package. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) keeps standing guidance because, for years, many families could not see the cost of each piece.

The FTC Funeral Rule applies to funeral providers in the United States. The core idea is simple: you have a right to see itemized prices before you buy. That list is not an insurance contract. It is a shopping tool. It can also help if you are considering final expense life insurance, because a policy benefit is measured in dollars — and those dollars need to cover what your family actually expects to pay.

What a funeral home must provide

If you ask in person, the funeral home must give you a General Price List, commonly called a GPL. The document lists the charge for each product and service, from the basic service fee to caskets, urns, and ceremony services.

You can also ask for prices by phone. You do not have to buy a complete package to get the items you actually need. The funeral home cannot require a casket for a direct cremation. You may use a casket or urn purchased elsewhere, according to the FTC’s rules.

These protections do not make a funeral cheap. They do not cap prices. They also do not turn the FTC into your insurance adviser. They mean you can compare line by line on a calmer day, instead of accepting the first package you are shown.

What the rule does not cover

The rule focuses on the funeral home. It generally does not cover cemeteries or sellers outside the establishment. The plot, outer burial container, monument, and some extras may appear on a different bill. If your family plan includes burial in a specific cemetery, write those costs separately. Otherwise a policy “the size of the funeral” can fall short.

The FTC can bring enforcement actions and civil penalties when there are violations. That does not pay a grieving family’s bill. That is why it helps to request the list now, not on the day of the service.

What this could mean for your family

Imagine a local list shows $2,100 for basic services, $1,400 for visitation, $1,200 for transfer and preparation, and $2,800 for a casket. The subtotal already exceeds $7,000 — and the cemetery is not included. If you had been thinking “about five thousand of insurance,” the list tells you that number came from an idea, not from written prices.

Final expense coverage is usually a small whole life policy. It does not pay the funeral home. After an eligible claim is approved, the company pays the death benefit to the beneficiary you name. That person decides how to use the money: funeral home, cemetery, medical bills, or another household need.

Does this cancel or change a policy you already have? No. An FTC guide does not modify your contract. Should you do something this week? If you still do not have a local list, asking for one is a concrete step. Does it matter if you are not buying a funeral today? Yes, if you are choosing a coverage amount: the amount should talk to real prices, not to a memory of “what an uncle’s funeral cost ten years ago.”

What you can do now

Request the GPL in writing or by phone. Compare two funeral homes, not just one. Mark which lines you would actually use and which you can skip. Add cemetery and extras separately. Bring that total into the insurance conversation — not the other way around.

Name a beneficiary and a backup name. A benefit does not help if no one knows who should collect it. Answer health questions on any application truthfully.

Frequently asked questions

Is the price list the same as final expense insurance?

No. The list shows what the funeral home charges. The insurance pays a cash benefit to the beneficiary, according to the contract.

Can I ask for prices without giving my name?

The FTC says you can request price information by phone. Use that option if it feels more comfortable.

Does the rule cover the cemetery?

Generally no. Write cemetery costs separately when you estimate how much coverage you might need.
