Don't guess: funeral prices, coverage, and a real term quote

September 6, 2026 · Guides for families | Mejor Vida Insurance | About 6 min read

This week: funeral price lists · how much coverage · a real term quote

Each week we explain life insurance and final expense topics in plain language. This edition covers three things families often mix up: how to use a funeral home’s written price list, why a national cost figure is not your family’s bill, and why a real term life quote is better than a guess.

Story 1: You can ask for a funeral price list. You do not have to buy a package.

Source: Federal Trade Commission | Published: September 6, 2026

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has a rule that protects families shopping for a funeral in the United States. If you ask in person, the funeral home must give you a written General Price List. That list shows each charge on its own line: the basic service fee, transporting the person who died, the visitation, a casket, or an urn.

You can also ask for prices by phone. You do not have to buy a full package if you only need a few services. A funeral home cannot make you buy a casket for a direct cremation.

These rules do not freeze prices. They also do not usually cover cemeteries or sellers outside the funeral home. They exist so you can compare one home with another. The same list helps if you are thinking about final expense life insurance. That insurance does not pay the funeral home. After an eligible claim, it pays a cash benefit to the person you name, according to the contract. With real prices in hand, it is easier to see how much coverage might be enough — and what is still left out.

Should you do anything this week? Ask for the written list before you buy. Does this change a policy you already have? No. A consumer rule about funeral homes does not change your insurance contract.

Story 2: A national cost figure is not your family’s funeral bill

Source: National Funeral Directors Association | Published: September 6, 2026

The National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA) publishes cost figures many families use as a starting point. In its 2023 study of funeral home price lists, the middle national price for a funeral with viewing and burial was $8,300. With viewing and cremation, the middle price was $6,280.

Those numbers are not your family’s bill. They often leave out a cemetery plot, a vault around the casket, a monument, flowers, travel, or a gathering after the service. The total changes with the city, the funeral home, and the choices you make.

Final expense coverage is usually a small whole life policy. It is not a prepaid funeral. After an eligible claim, the company pays the benefit to the beneficiary, who decides how to use the money. Use the national figures as context, then ask for a local price list. Add what you actually expect to pay, and compare that with money already set aside.

Does this affect you? Only as a planning starting point. Should you change a policy today because of a national number? No. How much coverage you can buy, what it costs, and whether you are approved still depend on the person, the product, and the company’s review of the application.

Story 3: A guess is not a term life quote

Source: LIMRA / Life Happens | Published: September 6, 2026

LIMRA and Life Happens have found that many people believe term life insurance costs far more than they later see on a quote. That research is not a price for you. It is a warning: a guess can make a family walk away without asking for real numbers.

Term life covers a set number of years, as long as the policy stays in force and its conditions are met. If the insured person dies during that time, the beneficiary may receive the death benefit. A quote uses information about a specific person. Even then, the price can change after the company reviews the application.

Price is not the same for everyone. It depends on age, health, tobacco use, the amount, the term length, the product, and the state. That is why a “best companies” list is not a quote. Before you decide it “costs too much,” decide how long your family may need the coverage and what you want it to pay for. Then ask for a quote with accurate information and read the conditions.

Does this mean term is cheap for you? No one can say that without your details. Is a policy you already have still okay? This research does not cancel it.
