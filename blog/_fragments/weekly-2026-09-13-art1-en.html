Life insurance applications rose in August, led by older adults

September 13, 2026 | Mejor Vida Insurance | About 10 min read

The MIB Life Index measured more applications in August, with the largest jump after age 50. An application is not an issued policy and not a price cut.

Published: September 13, 2026 · Source: see links at the end of the article

When a family puts off life insurance “for later,” they sometimes do it because they believe nobody applies after a certain age. August’s figures sketch a different picture. On September 10, InsuranceNewsNet reported that August 2026 life insurance application activity rose 18% from the same month in 2025. According to the MIB Life Index, that was the strongest year-over-year growth the index has recorded for any August.

The number matters, but it needs a calm reading. The MIB Life Index tracks applications among member life insurers. It does not publish what you would pay. It also does not count how many policies were issued. An application is the first step. Approval, amount, and premium come later — if they come at all.

What the index measured in August

Compared with July, activity barely moved: it fell 0.6%. The large jump is the year-over-year comparison. Every age group grew versus August 2025. The highest gains came after age 50: 22.9% for ages 50–59, 34.4% for 60–69, and 53.9% for 70 and older. Younger bands grew too, but by less.

Through the first eight months of 2026, overall activity was 15.6% higher than the same stretch of 2025. By product, term life applications rose 24.7%. Term covers a written period. Whole life, permanent coverage that generally builds cash value while the contract stays in force, rose 23.3%. Universal life — a permanent form whose premiums and financial pieces can be more flexible under the contract — rose 8.3%.

InsuranceNewsNet also noted that term grew in every age band, while whole life and universal life grew mainly among applicants 40 and older. That does not mean one product is “the right one” for your household. It means demand spread across more than one coverage type.

What the 18% does not say

It does not say premiums fell. It does not say every 70-year-old will be approved. It does not say your file will look like your neighbor’s. Health, tobacco, age, requested amount, carrier, and state still shape the review. An application index is a thermometer of interest, not a quote.

Imagine Rosa, 67. She reads that applications in her age band rose more than 30% and thinks, “then it must be easy now.” In practice her application still faces health questions and the company’s rules. August’s data tells her she is not alone in asking. It does not promise a yes.

What this could mean for your family

If someone depends on your income, or on unpaid work you do at home, waiting does not freeze the price. Age still counts when you apply. The rise among older adults shows many families are reviewing coverage later in life — not that the market became automatic.

Does this cancel a policy you already have? No. An application index does not change your contract. Should you buy this week because the number rose? Not because of the number alone. The useful step is to decide what the money is for — final expenses, income replacement, debts — and ask for information with your real details.

Final expense coverage is usually a small whole life policy. It does not pay the funeral home. After an eligible claim, it pays a cash benefit to the beneficiary you name. Term, by contrast, covers a set number of years. Neither product is “the MIB product.” The index only counts who is asking for paperwork.

What you can do now

Write down who would depend on you and for how many years. Separate a one-time cost, such as a funeral, from monthly bills that would continue. Ask for a quote with truthful information. Answer health questions honestly. Name a beneficiary and a backup name.

Frequently asked questions

Does the 18% mean premiums went down?

No. The index measures applications, not prices. Your premium still depends on your file and the company.

Is an application the same as an issued policy?

No. The application is the start. The company can still approve, postpone, limit the amount, or decline to offer coverage.

Is it too late to ask at age 70?

August’s data shows many people 70 and older did apply. That does not guarantee approval. Asking with real information is different from assuming there are no options.
