A guess is not a term life quote

September 6, 2026 | Mejor Vida Insurance | About 10 min read

LIMRA and Life Happens show that many people overestimate term cost. That is not a quote for you. Ask for a real quote and read the conditions.

Published: September 6, 2026 · Source: see links at the end of the article

A family sometimes rules out term life insurance in one sentence: “that’s way too expensive.” That sentence usually comes from a rumor, an ad, or an old memory. It rarely comes from a quote that uses the real name, age, and amount of the person who would be insured.

LIMRA and Life Happens have documented that perception gap. In the 2025 Insurance Barometer Study, healthy adults ages 18 to 30 sharply overestimated the cost of a 20-year level term policy for $250,000: their median estimate was about 10 to 12 times higher than the study’s reference cost. That finding is not your premium. It is evidence that an inflated idea can stop the conversation before it starts.

What a quote is — and what it is not

Term life covers a chosen period, subject to the policy’s conditions. If the insured person dies while the policy is in force and its terms are met, the beneficiary may receive the death benefit. It does not build cash value the way many whole life policies do.

A quote uses information about a specific applicant: age, stated health, tobacco use, amount, term length, and product. Even then, the price can change after underwriting. Underwriting is the insurer’s review of the application and risk. An initial figure is not a promise of approval or the final price.

Price is not the same for every person. It depends on the applicant, the company, the product type, the state, and evaluation rules. That is why a “cheapest companies” list does not replace a quote. A ranking does not have your file.

Term, whole life, and final expense

Term is often used to replace income for a set of years: while children are at home or a mortgage is being paid. Whole life is designed to last a lifetime if premiums are paid; at the same face amount it usually costs more per month. Final expense coverage is usually a smaller form of whole life for end-of-life costs, not a 20-year income replacement.

If your goal is “so my children do not inherit the funeral bill,” the product and amount are not the same as “replace my paycheck until the youngest finishes school.” Mixing those jobs into one imaginary policy creates frustration: you either overpay or under-cover.

What this could mean for your family

Imagine a 42-year-old parent who assumes a $250,000 term policy costs “like a second car.” That guess may be so high they never ask for numbers. The LIMRA perception study suggests that, for healthy younger adults, the gap between what people believe and the reference cost can be huge. At age 42 the price is different. The lesson still holds: do not close the door with a made-up number.

Does your current policy stay the same? Yes, if you keep paying it according to the contract. An article about cost perception does not cancel it. Should you do something? If you have delayed term coverage because “it must be expensive,” request a quote with real data. Does it matter if you are not 30? Yes: the trap of assuming is not only a young-adult problem.

What you can do now

Define the term length and amount around a concrete obligation. Gather age, tobacco use, and an honest health summary. Request a quote. Read what could change after underwriting. Compare term and whole life by the job each one should do, not by a headline.

Frequently asked questions

Is the LIMRA study my premium?

No. It describes a perception gap, especially for healthy adults 18 to 30 looking at a sample term policy. Your rate is something else.

Is an initial quote the final price?

Not always. It can change after underwriting. Treat it as a starting point, not a promise.

Should I pick the company from a ranking?

No. A ranking is not your file. Ask whether the company offers the amount and product you need.
