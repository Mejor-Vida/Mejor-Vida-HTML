1. U.S. Life Insurance Application Activity Begins 2026 with Strong Growth Across All Product Types

Source: MIB Group / LifeHealth | Published: February 10, 2026 | Read Full Article

Key Takeaways

  • Term Life applications surged 26.4% year-over-year in January 2026
  • Whole Life increased 15.6% compared to January 2025
  • Universal Life jumped 34.7%, showing the strongest growth
  • Triple-digit growth in applications from individuals aged 70+
  • Double-digit increases across ages 30-69
  • Follows record-breaking growth in 2025

The U.S. life insurance market has started 2026 with remarkable momentum, according to data from the MIB Group's Life Index. Application activity across all major product categories showed robust year-over-year growth, signaling continued strong consumer demand for life insurance protection.

The data reveals particularly impressive growth in Universal Life products, which jumped 34.7% compared to January 2025. Term Life applications, traditionally the most popular product for younger families, surged 26.4%, while Whole Life policies—often favored for their cash value accumulation and guaranteed death benefits—increased 15.6%.

Perhaps most striking is the demographic breakdown of this growth. Applications from individuals aged 70 and above showed triple-digit percentage increases, indicating heightened awareness among seniors about the importance of final expense planning and legacy protection. Meanwhile, the core working-age population (ages 30-69) demonstrated consistent double-digit growth across all age bands.

This strong performance follows an already record-breaking 2025, when the industry saw a 22% surge in December alone. Industry analysts attribute the continued momentum to several factors: increased consumer awareness of protection gaps, favorable economic conditions, and the maturation of digital underwriting processes that make obtaining coverage faster and more convenient.

Why This Matters

For final expense and life insurance agents, this trend indicates a highly receptive market with strong consumer awareness of the need for coverage. The surge in applications among seniors presents significant opportunities for final expense products, which are specifically designed to cover end-of-life costs and ease the financial burden on families. The broad-based growth across age groups suggests favorable conditions for building a diverse client base throughout 2026. Agents should be prepared for increased consumer inquiries and ensure they have the tools and training to efficiently process applications while maintaining high service standards.

2. Oklahoma Adopts NAIC 2025 Valuation Manual for Life and Health Insurers

Source: Oklahoma Insurance Department / NAIC | Published: February 11, 2026 | Read Full Article

Key Takeaways

  • Effective January 1, 2026 in Oklahoma
  • 97.81% of premiums represented by adopting states
  • 50 of 55 NAIC members approved the amendments
  • Establishes new policy valuation standards
  • Strengthens insurer financial stability
  • No phased implementation—full effect immediately

Oklahoma Insurance Commissioner Glen Mulready confirmed on February 11, 2026, that the National Association of Insurance Commissioners' (NAIC) 2025 Valuation Manual amendments became effective in Oklahoma on January 1, 2026. This regulatory update represents a significant step forward in ensuring the financial stability of life and health insurance companies operating in the state.

The amendments were approved by 50 of the NAIC's 55 member jurisdictions in August 2024, representing an overwhelming 97.81% of applicable premiums written across the United States. This near-universal adoption demonstrates the insurance industry's commitment to enhanced financial standards and consumer protection.

The Valuation Manual establishes the methodologies and standards that life and health insurers must use to value their policy liabilities. These valuations are critical because they directly influence an insurer's reported financial position and their capacity to meet future obligations to policyholders. The 2025 amendments update these standards to reflect current economic conditions, mortality trends, and risk management practices.

Unlike some regulatory changes that include transitional periods or phased implementation, Oklahoma's adoption took full effect immediately on January 1, 2026. The notice applies to all life and accident and health insurers operating in Oklahoma, with companies directed to contact their assigned financial analyst for any clarification questions.

Why This Matters

While this is a technical regulatory change, it has important practical implications for both agents and consumers. The updated valuation standards strengthen the financial foundation of insurance companies, which translates to greater confidence in their long-term stability and ability to pay claims. For agents, this provides an important talking point when discussing carrier strength with clients—the widespread adoption (97.81% of premiums) demonstrates industry-wide commitment to enhanced financial standards. For consumers, especially those purchasing final expense or permanent life insurance policies that may not pay out for decades, this regulatory framework provides additional assurance that their chosen carrier will be there when their beneficiaries need it most.

3. Brendan Forsyth Introduces Structured Agent Development Model for Life Insurance Industry

Source: Industry Training Initiative | Published: February 13, 2026 | Read Full Article

Key Takeaways

  • Formalizes training pathways for agent career development
  • Establishes clear performance expectations and competency benchmarks
  • Emphasizes licensing, field training, and documentation standards
  • Includes recurring evaluations and integrated mentorship
  • Reduces variability in field operations
  • Reinforces compliance and regulatory alignment

On February 13, 2026, industry leader Brendan Forsyth announced a comprehensive structured model for life insurance agent development, designed to formalize training pathways, performance expectations, and competency benchmarks throughout an agent's career. This initiative represents a significant step toward standardizing professional development in the life insurance distribution channel.

The framework addresses a long-standing challenge in the insurance industry: the wide variability in agent training and development across different agencies and organizations. By establishing clear stages of professional development, the model provides agents with a roadmap for career advancement while ensuring they maintain the knowledge and skills necessary to serve clients effectively.

Key components of the structured model include:

  • Licensing Requirements: Clear pathways for obtaining and maintaining necessary state licenses
  • Field Training: Hands-on experience with experienced mentors
  • Documentation Standards: Consistent record-keeping and compliance practices
  • Recurring Evaluations: Regular assessments to ensure ongoing competency
  • Integrated Mentorship: Pairing newer agents with experienced professionals

The model emphasizes not just product knowledge, but also regulatory compliance, ethical sales practices, and client-centered service. This holistic approach aims to reduce the variability in field operations that can lead to compliance issues and poor client outcomes.

Why This Matters

For life insurance professionals, this structured approach provides a clear roadmap for career advancement and skill development. Agencies that adopt this model can expect more consistent agent performance, reduced compliance risks, and improved client outcomes. For consumers, this means working with better-trained agents who follow established best practices, leading to more appropriate product recommendations and smoother policy administration. The emphasis on mentorship and ongoing evaluation helps ensure the industry maintains high professional standards, which ultimately benefits everyone involved. As the industry continues to evolve with new products and technologies, having a structured development framework becomes increasingly important for maintaining quality and professionalism.

4. Continental General Insurance Acquires 91,000 Policies from State Guaranty Associations

Source: Continental General Insurance Company | Published: February 12, 2026 | Read Full Article

Key Takeaways

  • 91,000 policies acquired from state guaranty associations
  • Includes life insurance, annuities, and health policies
  • Originally issued by Bankers Life Insurance Company (BLIC) and Colorado Bankers Life Insurance Company (CBLIC)
  • Ensures continuity of coverage after 2024 liquidation
  • Many policyholders hold final expense and supplemental health coverage
  • Demonstrates industry commitment to policyholder protection

Continental General Insurance Company completed a significant acquisition on February 12, 2026, taking over approximately 91,000 life insurance, annuity, and health policies from state guaranty associations. These policies were originally issued by Bankers Life Insurance Company (BLIC) and Colorado Bankers Life Insurance Company (CBLIC) before their liquidation in 2024.

Executive Chairman Michael Gorzynski emphasized that this acquisition ensures continuity of coverage and specialized administration for affected policyholders. Many of these policies are final expense and supplemental health products, which serve vulnerable populations who depend on this coverage for financial security and peace of mind.

The transaction demonstrates how the insurance industry's safety net system works in practice. When an insurance company fails, state guaranty associations step in to protect policyholders, ensuring that coverage continues and claims are paid. These associations then work to transfer the policies to financially stable carriers who can provide ongoing administration and service.

Continental General, with its expertise in final expense and supplemental health insurance, was well-positioned to take on these policies. The company's specialized knowledge in these product lines means policyholders can expect knowledgeable service and efficient claims processing going forward.

Why This Matters

This acquisition provides critical reassurance to consumers and agents that life insurance policies remain protected even in worst-case scenarios. The state guaranty association system, combined with acquisitions like this, ensures that policyholders don't lose their coverage when an insurer fails. For final expense agents, this is an important talking point when addressing client concerns about carrier stability—the industry has multiple safety nets in place. The successful transfer of 91,000 policies also highlights the ongoing consolidation in the insurance market and the importance of working with financially strong carriers. Agents should be prepared to explain how guaranty associations work and reassure clients that their coverage is protected by more than just the issuing carrier's financial strength.

5. UK Prudential Regulation Authority Explores Alternative Capital Options for Life Insurers

Source: Prudential Regulation Authority (UK) | Published: February 6, 2026 | Read Full Article

Key Takeaways

  • Consultation period closed February 6, 2026
  • Exploring Insurance Linked Securities (ILS)
  • Examining Synthetic Risk Transfers
  • Considering life insurance sidecars
  • Feedback statement expected H1 2026
  • Reflects global trends in insurance capital management

The UK's Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) closed its consultation period on February 6, 2026, for a discussion paper exploring how life insurers can access alternative forms of capital beyond traditional equity or debt issuances. While this is a UK-focused initiative, it reflects broader global trends in insurance capital management and risk transfer mechanisms that are increasingly relevant to U.S. insurers as well.

The PRA is examining several innovative structures:

  • Insurance Linked Securities (ILS): Financial instruments that transfer insurance risk to capital market investors
  • Synthetic Risk Transfers: Mechanisms that transfer risk without transferring the underlying assets
  • Life Insurance Sidecars: Special purpose vehicles that allow insurers to share risk with third-party investors

These alternative capital structures can help insurers manage risk more efficiently, access capital during periods of growth, and potentially reduce the cost of capital. The PRA's exploration of these options aims to facilitate innovation while maintaining prudential standards that protect policyholders.

A feedback statement and potential policy proposals are expected in the first half of 2026, which will provide more clarity on how the UK regulatory framework might evolve to accommodate these alternative capital structures.

Why This Matters

Although this development is specific to the UK market, it signals broader industry trends toward innovative capital structures and risk management tools that may eventually influence U.S. regulatory approaches. For U.S. life insurance professionals, understanding these global developments provides context for domestic regulatory discussions around reinsurance, capital requirements, and alternative risk transfer mechanisms. The exploration of alternative capital sources could lead to more efficient capital deployment, potentially benefiting policyholders through more competitive pricing and product innovation. As the global insurance market becomes increasingly interconnected, developments in major markets like the UK often foreshadow similar discussions in the United States. Agents and agencies should stay informed about these trends as they may impact carrier strategies, product availability, and pricing in the coming years.
