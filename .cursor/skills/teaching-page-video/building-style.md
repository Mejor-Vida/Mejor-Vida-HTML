## Clarity first, length second

Do not telegraph a lesson to hit 90–180 seconds.

- Write the thought a first-time viewer needs: who, what, why it sounds similar, how it is different, what to do.
- If the clear draft runs long, **say the word count** and wait. Trim only after Julie likes the wording.
- Do not “tighten” by deleting the setup sentence. That is what made the prepaid draft fail.
- Skip extra **topics** (SSI/Medicaid, NFDA medians, full comparison charts, a second lesson of mix-ups) to save time. Do not skip **context** on the topics you kept.
- After Julie likes the wording, she may still reject runtime. The familiares long take was ~7 minutes; she called that too long. Cut extra *topics*, not the setup. **Cap: under 4 minutes** unless she accepts a longer wording first. The funeral-cost draft (~419 words, ~3:10) is the length to aim at. Do not “save time” by turning the story into an outline, a webpage tour, or a college-paper preview of three headings.

## Name the thing, every time it would be unclear

A viewer who joined mid-sentence must still know the noun.

| Do not say | Say |
| --- | --- |
| some contracts / some plans / that home / they / it | some prepaid contracts with a funeral home / that funeral home / the family / the money you paid |
| the list / those lists | who the company will accept as owner, and who it will accept only to receive the money |
| Where your money sits is state law | What happens to your money after you pay the funeral home is based on state law, not federal law |
| You are buying the lines named in the contract | You are buying specific items from that funeral home’s written menu — then give examples, then name the general price list |

If two legal rules sit next to each other, say both sides. Example: the FTC rule covers **how you shop**. What happens to **the money after you pay the funeral home** is based on **state law, not federal law**.

## Name the class, then the examples

If the page is about a **class** of people or products, that class is the lesson. One search query or one FAQ is an example, not the title of the video.

**Rejected:** a whole script framed as “can I insure an uncle?” because that was the top Google query.

**Locked:** other relatives who are not a parent, spouse, or child — an aunt, an uncle, a cousin, an in-law, a niece, a nephew.

## YouTube does not have the webpage (locked)

The viewer may never open the site. Take **facts** from the teaching page. Do not talk about the page. Do not organize the script as a walk through the article.

- Do not say “this page,” “on this page,” “as you see here,” “esta página,” or “los montos de muestra de esta página.”
- Do not walk the article H2s in order. The breakdown spine is for extracting facts. The spoken script is a YouTube story from the page’s **main idea** — something a stranger could search and follow with the website closed.
- Skip anything that only makes sense if you already saw the page: sample premium tabs, rate tables, “use those other guides,” quote-rail, TOC.
- A company example is “here is one example, so this is not abstract,” not “this page uses Mutual of Omaha.”
- Sample face amounts and table cells stay off camera unless they are a public figure with a year and a source the viewer can understand without the site.

## Jobs before labels

If a term is a company rule, say the **job** first. Then give the name.

**Rejected:** “What trips people up is the next step. Even when that interest is there, the company’s owner list and its beneficiary list are not the same list.” A YouTube viewer does not know what “list” means.

**Locked:** two questions. First, will the company insure that person at all (insurable interest). Second, will they let *you* control the contract. Then: the insured person signs; the owner runs the contract and is who the company talks to; paying the premium does not make you the owner; the beneficiary receives the check; a contingent beneficiary is only a backup.

Do not say “the list” (or “la lista”) until those jobs are already clear — and prefer never saying it. Say “who the company will accept as owner” and “who it will accept only to receive the money.”

## Two questions, not a webpage tour

When people mix two things that feel the same, organize the video around **those two questions**, not around the page’s sections.

Familiares model: it *feels* like covering a parent (you would pay the funeral, so you want a policy). On the company’s side it is (1) can this person be insured at all, and (2) can you own the policy. Recap those same two questions at the close.

Same pattern as prepaid: say what people are trying to do, name path A, name path B, say why they sound alike, say they are not the same, say what each is tied to, say when the difference shows up.

## Speak the idea; do not paste the webpage

Page takeaways are slogans. Speech has to unpack them.

**Webpage (do not read this):** “You are not buying a generic funeral. You are buying the lines named in the contract.”

**Speech:** “When you prepay, you are not buying ‘a funeral’ as one bundled product. You are buying specific items from that funeral home’s written menu — things like the transfer of the body, a visitation, staff, and a casket or an urn. That menu is called the general price list…”

## When two paths sound alike

Do not stack four short punches and hope the viewer infers the difference.

**Too thin (rejected):** “One path is to sign with a funeral home now. Another is to leave cash to someone you trust. Those sound alike. They are not the same contract if the family moves, the plan changes, or the business closes.”

**First-draft style:** Say what people are trying to do. Name path A. Name path B. Say **why** they sound alike. Say they are **not** the same thing. Say what each one is actually tied to. Then say **when** the difference shows up.

## Sentence music

Short and medium sentences are fine. A string of five-word sentences is an outline, not speech.

Before handing the script over, read it out loud. Fix:

- Webpage / chatbot / telegram cadence
- “Some contracts” with no funeral home
- “The list” / “this page” / a table the viewer cannot see
- A contrast that never says why the two things get confused
- A recap that only repeats a slogan
- One example standing in for a whole class (uncle for all other relatives)
